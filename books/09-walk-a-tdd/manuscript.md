# 0. How to use this book

This book is for the person sent to a data center to say, in writing, what a lender or a buyer may rely on. The visit is a technical due diligence. It is not a design. It is not a commissioning. It is not an audit of the tenant's applications.

The method comes from a lender's technical visit on a live hall: desk review, a questionnaire, interviews, a walk, a count, a sample, and a witness of tests someone else ran. The site, the parties, the addresses, and the equipment list stay out. If a sentence here starts to look like that report, it does not belong.

The earlier books already say when a file is a pass, when a screen may speak, and when a receiver refuses a signature. This book is the visit that produces the sentence those people will be asked to trust. You do not rerun their heat window. You do not sign their operation. You write what you saw, what you accepted as a document, and what you did not look at.

When you finish, you should be able to hand over one visit sheet. It names the scope, the chairs, the sample rule, and the sentence you will sign. It is in chapter 14. If you fly tomorrow, start there. The week before is chapter 26. A visit with no site, so the sheet has a shape, is chapter 30. Conditions someone will draft from your findings are chapter 31.

## What you may copy

You may copy the sheet headings. You may not paste a sentence from this book into a customer's report and call it their finding. Their contract, their drawings, and their serial lists stay in their file.

# 1. What this visit is

A technical due diligence is a reading of technical risk against a contract, done by someone who does not own the plant and will not operate it. The reader is usually a lender, sometimes a buyer. They need to know whether the thing they are financing can do the job the papers say, and what happens if a named assumption fails. They do not need a second design, and they do not need you to be enthusiastic.

Three principles are enough. You are independent of the parties. You follow risk to a delivery node or to later operation, not every interesting detail. You check compliance by citing the title the contract already names, not by pasting the standard. If a finding does not change a delivery date, a contracted performance, a permit, or the ability to operate, it is a note. Notes may be filed. They do not get the tone of a red flag.

You will be asked to certify things the financing documents name. Read that list before you travel. A certification you did not see in the engagement is not yours to invent on site. A certification you cannot evidence is refused, in the same document as any yes. The handover book already required that shape. It applies here with a lender in the room instead of an operator.

## What the visit is not

It is not a market essay. A paragraph about regional demand, geopolitics, or how competitive the platform "feels" is not a technical finding. If the engagement asks for obsolescence risk, you write what the contract assumes about the equipment generation, and whether a supply agreement covers a shortfall. You do not write a forecast. Counsel reads the warranty. You say whether the technical assumption inside it is evidenced.

It is not a commissioning. You do not issue levels. You may read a level map if one exists, and say whether the words match the tests. The third book owns how those levels are numbered. You do not reteach them in the lender's report.

It is not a safety inspection you are not qualified to sign, and it is not a legal opinion. Permits are confirmed with counsel, from the documents they accept. You do not bless a filing because the cover looks official.

## The four-stage review and the risk taxonomy

Every technical due diligence operates along a disciplined four-stage pipeline. Conflating stages or delivering an informal verdict at the curb destroys the legal insulation of the technical opinion:

1. **Stage 1: Desk Review and Virtual Data Room (VDR) Audit**: Inspect utility interconnection agreements, firm capacity reservations, Single-Line Diagrams (SLD), civil hydrology studies, building permits, Certificate of Occupancy, Phase I Environmental Site Assessments (ESA), and equipment maintenance logs.
2. **Stage 2: Physical Site Walk and Asset Verification**: Execute the four core acts—Count against inventory, sample identity under the pre-written rule (N_sample = √N + 1), sample running operational state, and verify test documentation without confusing pass certificates with witnessed tests.
3. **Stage 3: Interim Red-Flag Report (48-Hour Turnaround)**: Deliver an early, numbered ledger identifying deal-breakers, unmitigated single points of failure (SPOF), and material capital expenditure (CAPEX) catch-up requirements.
4. **Stage 4: Final Technical Opinion and Financing Covenants**: Formulate enforceable Conditions Precedent (CP) required prior to initial debt drawdown, Conditions Subsequent (CS) post-closing cure milestones, and formal escrow holdbacks (150%–200% of remediation cost).

![Figure 09.1: Technical Due Diligence Audit Framework and Fatal Flaw Gating](../figures/fig-01-tdd-audit-framework-and-red-flags.png)

Findings are rigorously segregated into a three-tier hierarchy:
- **Tier 1: Fatal Flaw (Red Flag)**: Unresolvable utility clawbacks, location within unmitigated 100-year/500-year flood plains, lack of fundamental zoning or occupancy permits, unresolvable right-of-way encumbrances, or total absence of emergency autonomous run paths. Halts debt syndication immediately.
- **Tier 2: Material Deficiency (Amber Flag)**: Degraded fuel without polishing, floor loading deficits for dense AI racks, single-header cooling vulnerabilities, or incomplete integrated systems commissioning. Handled via mandatory Conditions Precedent and financial escrow holdbacks.
- **Tier 3: Operational Defect (Green Flag)**: Redline drawing reconciliation backlog, minor maintenance contract renewal gaps, or cosmetic non-structural defects. Resolved under Conditions Subsequent within a 90-day post-closing cure window.

# 2. The line you will not cross

A hall visit collects everything, because everything is in the room. Your scope does not. Write the line before the questionnaire goes out. For an operator who delivers infrastructure and a hardware platform, the line often stops under the tenant's operating system. Applications, the tenant's own security operation, and the management of their jobs are not your audit, unless the engagement names them in a sentence you can evidence.

Crossing the line feels thorough. It makes the report wider than the access you had. A screenshot of a dashboard is not an application audit. A conversation about how the customer trains models is not a finding about the plant. File the conversation as context if you must. Do not let it support a sentence about fitness.

The same line runs the other way. The room provider's electrical plant is not the operator's spare-parts drawer, and the operator's drawer is not the tenant's acceptance test. Each chair has papers. You do not borrow a pass from one chair to clear another. Chapter 3 names the chairs. This chapter only says you write the line, and you repeat it in the report so a later reader cannot widen it.

If the engagement is silent, ask before you travel. Silence is not permission to audit the tenant. It is a missing scope. A missing scope is refused the way a missing sheet is refused. You may still walk what you can see. You may not call the unseen layers reviewed.

## When they widen it for you

The party under review will offer more access than the engagement, because more access feels like a cleaner opinion. A tenant screen, a security log, a job history. Take it only if you are willing to evidence it and the reader asked. Extra access you do not know how to judge will be described badly, and the description will be treated as a review. Decline, and write declined. Declined is cleaner than a paragraph of impressions.

If the reader later wishes the line had been wider, that is a new engagement, with access and a method, not a sentence you add because you happened to see a screen. Happened to see is not a sample rule. It is how one machine becomes "the platform."

# 3. Three chairs

Someone provides the room: power, cooling, the building, the duty roster for the hall. Someone operates the equipment that was put in the room: servers, the network they own, the spares, the tickets. Someone is the customer who specified the equipment and may run the work on it. Three chairs. A due diligence that merges them will sign one party's pass for another's risk.

Write who delivers what, by function, not by brand. The room can be excellent and the equipment count wrong. The equipment can be counted and the tenant's test unwitnessed. The tenant can have a pass document and the operator can have no spare. Each of those is a separate sentence.

Ask, for each contracted performance, which chair owes it. A service level that the end customer cares about may depend on the room provider's power, the operator's spare, and the customer's own acceptance. If you cannot name the chair, you cannot say the level is achievable. You can say the papers do not show who owes it. That is a finding.

Do not take a market story from one chair and attach it to the plant. Fitness for a later tenant, a change of use, a retail shape instead of a lease, is a commercial scenario. It is not evidenced by a walk. If the engagement requires you to note it, label it a scenario. Scenarios do not get the verb "can."

# 4. Independence

You are not on the project team. You do not fix a finding during the visit in order to clear it. You do not let the party who wants the financing draft your sentence. Independence is a practical rule: your name is not on their certificate, their payroll for this asset, or their punch-list close.

A questionnaire answered only by the party under review is their claim. It becomes your finding only when a document or a walk agrees, and you write which. An interview is a claim with a voice. Same rule. Desk review without a walk can support a paper finding. It cannot support "we saw the plant." Say which visit you made.

If you are shown a draft of your own report with their edits already in the findings, read it as a comment, not as your text. Restore the sentence you can evidence, or refuse the paragraph. Attendance at their meeting is not agreement. Do not leave your name on a circulated draft you did not sign.

Gifts, a tour that skips the rooms in scope, and a data room that opens the day you fly are limits, not insults. Write the limit. A finding of "no issue" requires the look. If the look was narrowed, the no-issue sentence narrows with it.

# 5. Papers before the plane

The visit is short. The reading is not. Ask for the papers before you travel, with enough days to actually read them. A data room that opens on the morning of the walk is a tour. You may still walk. You may not sign a review of documents you have not read.

The minimum set, if the engagement is a lender's technical look at a delivered hall:

The engagement, and the certifications it names. The contract that states the performance, cited, not pasted. The split of chairs: who provides the room, who operates, who the customer is. Permits the counsel will rely on, listed, so you know which ones you are not opining on. The level map or test pack, if they claim commissioning. The as-operated drawings only far enough to know design from as-built. A duty roster. A spare rule. The procedures a night shift can find: standing, change, and emergency. Not the whole binder. The index, and the three you will sample.

You do not demand a market study, a serial dump, or a point list. A serial dump is their asset file. Your sheet will say whether identity was checked, and the rule, not the numbers. A point list is not a quality mark. The monitor book already refused that substitution.

If a paper is missing, write missing before you fly, so the site visit is not spent hunting it. A paper produced on site is read later, or it is marked unread. Unread is not accepted.

## What you do with a late room

If the room opens after you have traveled, you do not pretend the week of reading happened on site. You list what you could read in the hours you had, and you list the rest as not read. A speed-read of a contract on a phone is not a review of the contract. Say skimmed, or say not read. Skimmed does not support a sentence about a clause you might have missed. The clauses that match the performance sentence are either opened, or the performance review waits.

Do not let the host walk you through the documents instead of giving them to you. A walkthrough is an interview about the documents. It is not the documents. Ask for the file. If the file cannot leave a screen they control, read it there and write the revision and the time. A screen they scroll for you, too fast to read, is not read.

# 6. Ask, then check

Three tools, used in that order. A questionnaire makes them write the claim. An interview shows whether the person who operates can say the same claim. A desk review checks the claim against a document. None of the three is a walk. Do not let a confident interview replace a count.

The questionnaire asks for functions, not for a story. Who is on at night. What spare the job will not start without. Which contract sentence they believe they meet. What they know is open. A questionnaire that only asks for strengths will be answered with strengths. Ask for the open items by name. If they write none, the punch print later either agrees or the questionnaire is a false document.

The interview is with the person who would be called, not only the person who sells the financing. If that person cannot say the first safe state, the emergency paper is not issued, whatever the binder contains. You do not grade their character. You record whether the stop could be said aloud.

Desk review cites the document, the revision, and the date you read it. A slide with no revision is not the document. A contract appendix you did not open is not reviewed. Write "not read" rather than a smooth paragraph that implies you did.

Disagreement between the three is the finding. Do not average them into a likely story. The handover book refused that average. So does this visit.

# 7. The walk

On a short delivery you will not retest the hall. You can still do four things that are not a tour. Count. Identify. Sample. Witness.

Count is everything in scope, against their list, or a statement that the list was not there and you counted what you could see. A count without their list is an inventory, not a reconciliation. Say which. Do not publish the list. Their identifiers stay in their file. Your note says the totals agreed, or where they did not, by function.

Identify is a sample of identity, written before you arrive. How many, from which rows, compared to the asset record: present, matching, or not. A rule invented in the aisle to match what you had time for is not a rule. If you check fewer than you wrote, the sentence narrows. It does not stay "identity verified."

Sample the running state the same way. One or two machines in a row, configuration against the record, and whether it was actually running. A dark machine is not a pass because the row looked full. Write dark. Do not infer health from a front panel.

Witness means you saw their test, or you saw the document that says it passed, and you say which. Their pass document is not your test. The verification book already split those verbs. Use them. If you did not see the test, you do not write that you verified performance. You write that a document exists, who signed it, and that you did not rerun it.

Photographs are evidence in their file if the engagement allows them. They are not the finding. A photo of a nameplate is not a load. A photo of a screen is not a quality mark.

## What you write the same day

Write the four acts before you sleep, in the sheet, not in a chat. Count result. Sample as written or narrowed. What was dark. Which verb for their test. Rooms not entered. A note written the next afternoon from memory will promote the rooms you understood and drop the door that stayed shut. The shut door is the point of writing it the same day.

Do not add serials to that note if the note will be pasted into the opinion. Function and result are enough. Their asset file already has the identities. A working note that only makes sense beside a photograph is a caption. Rewrite it in sentences before you leave the building, while you can still check a door.

If they ask for a verbal result at the door, give the limits, not a preview of the yes. "Count done, one identity gap, performance not witnessed, second feed spoken only." That is a complete door sentence. "Looks fine" is the sentence you will spend the next week taking back.

# 8. The plant, as classes of risk

You are not redrawing the single line. You are asking whether the class of risk the contract assumes is the class you can see, and whether a failure of that class has a path that does not depend on a speech.

Power: is there a second path for the equipment in scope, and is it evidenced by a drawing you accept as current plus a test document, or only by a speech? A ring, a second feed, a generator, a battery duration, are names. They become findings only when a document says they were tested, or you saw the test. A duration printed on a UPS brochure is not the test. Design kilowatts are not the operating load. The day book already refused that cell. Do not fill it here to look complete.

Cooling: condensate that can reach equipment is a risk you can see. Makeup water that has one source and no stored volume is a risk you can ask about. You do not need their pipe sizes in the report. You need the path, and whether a second source or a tank is a document or a hope.

Controls and leaks of air and water follow the same split. A screen that is green is not a handover of the monitor. If the financing depends on alarms, the points that would wake someone need the acceptance the monitor book already defined, or you write that the screen is installed and not accepted.

Write risks as: what fails, who owes the response, what you saw, what is only a document. A risk with no owner is the finding. A risk they call minor is not sized by the adjective. If it can stop the contracted performance, it is not minor.

## Emergency fuel and water autonomy envelopes

A facility that claims Tier III or Tier IV concurrently maintainable autonomy must maintain physical, unshared on-site storage envelopes capable of sustaining 100% full-load operations during a catastrophic grid or municipal water collapse:

![Figure 09.4: On-Site Fuel and Water Emergency Autonomy Envelopes](../figures/fig-04-onsite-fuel-and-water-autonomy-envelope.png)

### 1. Emergency diesel storage and fuel quality physics

The total fuel storage envelope must satisfy a minimum 72-hour full-load runtime under continuous emergency generation, augmented by an industry-standard 15% safety ullage margin:

$$V_{\text{fuel}} = N_{\text{gen}} \times \dot{m}_{\text{fuel}} \times t_{\text{run}} \times 1.15$$

For a 20 MW IT facility supported by 10 standby diesel generators (2.5 MVA each, consuming approximately 540 L/h at full load), the mandatory 72-hour on-site reserve is:

$$V_{\text{fuel}} = 10 \times 540\,\text{L/h} \times 72\,\text{h} \times 1.15 = 447,120\,\text{liters} \quad (\approx 448\,\text{m}^3)$$

A bulk tank alone does not guarantee electrical survival. Modern Ultra-Low Sulfur Diesel (ULSD) is hygroscopic and degrades rapidly in quiescent underground tanks (UST). Condensation creates a water-hydrocarbon interface where anaerobic microbes (*Cladosporium resinae*) flourish, metabolizing sulfur and generating asphaltic particulate sludge. 

During a grid blackout, fuel transfer pumps draw this bottom sludge directly into engine fuel rails. High-pressure common rail (HPCR) diesel injectors operating at 2,000 bar foul and seize within 10 to 15 minutes of startup.

**The TDD Audit Verification**:
1. Check for an automated duplex fuel polishing system operating 24/7. It must incorporate a primary centrifugal coalescer stripping free/emulsified water down to < 50 ppm (ASTM D2709), followed by a 2-micron particulate filter.
2. Confirm the system achieves 100% tank volume turnover every 30 to 45 days.
3. Draw a physical bottom thief sample from each bulk tank during the walk. If the fuel is cloudy, dark, or exhibits biological particulate matter, log a mandatory Tier 2 Condition Precedent requiring full laboratory ASTM D975 fuel assay and remediation prior to closing.

### 2. Evaporative cooling water and thermal ride-through buffer

Evaporative cooling towers reject approximately 1.5–2.2 liters of makeup water per kilowatt-hour of heat rejection (accounting for evaporation, drift, and cycles of concentration blowdown). A 20 MW facility consumes up to 864,000 liters per day (864 m³/day). The TDD auditor must verify a certified 48-hour on-site bulk raw water storage reserve (≥ 1,728 m³) or two independent, geographically diverse municipal water utility mains entering from separate rights-of-way.

Furthermore, centrifugal chillers require 7 to 12 minutes (t_restart) to restart compressors and establish refrigerant oil differential pressure following an electrical transfer transient. In high-density AI clusters, IT liquid loops heat up at rates exceeding +0.15°C/second; without active thermal buffering, coolant supply temperatures breach maximum limits (>35°C) in less than 90 seconds.

The required stratified chilled water Thermal Energy Storage (TES) volume is governed by:

$$V_{\text{tes}} = \frac{Q_{\text{cooling}} \times t_{\text{restart}}}{\rho \times C_p \times \Delta T}$$

Where:
- Q_cooling is the peak critical cooling load (kW).
- t_restart is the certified chiller restart and pull-down latency (seconds).
- ρ is water density (1,000 kg/m³).
- C_p is specific heat capacity (4.186 kJ/kg·°C).
- ΔT is the allowable temperature rise across the thermal buffer before IT thermal throttling occurs (typically 4–6°C).

If the facility lacks an inline stratified TES tank or continuous secondary loop volume sized to this equation, log an unmitigated thermal single point of failure.

## Decisions to record

- Power path: tested, documented, or spoken. Spoken is not accepted.
- 72-hour diesel storage volume reconciled against generator burn rates and 1.15 safety margin.
- Duplex fuel polishing system installed, running, and documented against ASTM D2709 / ASTM D975.
- Water makeup autonomy: 48-hour on-site bulk storage evidenced or diverse utility mains verified.
- Chilled water thermal storage buffer (TES) sized for full chiller restart duration.
- Alarms the financing depends on: accepted as points, or installed only.
- Design load kept out of the operating cell.

# 9. People, paper, spares

A plant does not meet a service level because the equipment is redundant on a slide. It meets it if someone is there, the stop is findable, and the part the job refuses to start without is in a place a second person can find.

The roster is a document, not a name they mention. Who covers the hour the contract cares about. A team name is not a person. If the roster and the interview disagree, write both. Do not pick the kinder one.

Procedures: you sample, you do not certify the binder. Standing, change, and emergency, one of each if they exist. Can the person say the stop. Does the emergency paper end at the first safe state, or is it a repair manual. A twenty-page emergency paper is a finding about the paper, not a compliment about thoroughness. The day book already split those three. Use the split. Do not paste their procedures.

Spares: the rule, not the catalog. Which job will not start without which part, and whether that part was seen or only listed. A list you did not check is their claim. A price list is not a spare. Training is a signature that the pages were shown, not a slide attendance sheet, if they claim the staff can run the platform.

Maintenance policy and lifespan are claims about the future. You may note the manufacturer's stated life if a document says it, and you say you did not test the life. You may not convert a depreciation period in a contract into a technical life. Those are different sentences. Counsel owns the first. You refuse to dress it as the second.

# 11. What you do not calculate

A due diligence accumulates numbers because lenders like numbers. Most of them are not yours.

You do not compute a failure rate for equipment that has no rate in a document you may cite, and then multiply it into a probability that three will fail together. That product looks like engineering. It is a story. If the manufacturer has not published a figure you are allowed to use, you do not borrow one from "industry practice." Industry practice is the word the handover book already refused. The practical control is the spare, the procedure, and the roster. Write those. Leave the probability out.

You do not convert a nameplate, a design kilowatt, or a brochure runtime into the operating case. You do not add them. You do not fill a capacity cell to seem complete.

You do not score the team. "Technically strong" is a mood. The evidence is whether they could say the stop, whether the sample matched the record, and whether the open items were on the page. Moods do not survive a question from counsel.

You do not invent a sample percentage in this book for every visit. The visit writes its own rule before arrival and follows it. A rule copied from a previous report, including one you wrote, is another hall. State this visit's rule.

You do not put a market growth rate, a share, or a competitiveness adjective in the technical opinion. If the engagement forces a market section, it is a separate section, labeled not a finding, and you do not let the opinion cite it.

## A theory of failure is still a theory

You will be tempted by a short calculation. Take a figure you half remember for how often a class of machine fails. Multiply by the count. Multiply again for the chance that several fail at once. The product is small, so the service level looks safe. The product is not evidence. You do not have their failure figure. A borrowed figure is not a test. A small product will be quoted as your engineering judgment that simultaneous failure can be ignored. That is the sentence this chapter exists to stop.

The control that belongs in the opinion is ordinary and checkable. Is there a spare for the failure the contract assumes. Is there a person. Is there a paper that stops at a safe state. Did you see them, or only hear them. Those three sentences do the work the probability was meant to do, without inventing a rate. If any of the three is missing, the service level is not evidenced. You do not need a tiny number to say that. A tiny number would hide it.

# 13. When the window is short

Some deliveries give you days, not a season. The method does not become a tour because the window is short. It becomes a narrower sentence.

Do the four walk acts, smaller, and say so. A full count if the list exists. An identity sample you can finish, under a rule you wrote that morning only if the engagement allows a rule that late. Prefer the rule from the night before. A running-state sample, a few machines, not a feeling about the row. Witness of the customer's test, or acceptance of their pass document, with the verb accurate. If you cannot stay for the test, you have the document or you have nothing. Do not reconstruct the test from a conversation.

Drop, explicitly, what the window cannot support. You did not rerun commissioning. You did not audit the operating system. You did not measure power quality. You did not establish a failure rate. The report that pretends those happened is the false document. A short true report is the work.

Prioritize by the contract sentence. If the financing depends on the equipment being the equipment they bought, identity and count come before a tour of the cooling plant. If it depends on the room staying up, the power path comes before the market slide. A short window spent on the interesting room is a failed visit even if the notes are long.

Write the hours you had, and the rooms you did not enter. Unentered is not reviewed. A host who fills the hours with presentations has used the window. Ask for the floor. If you do not get it, the opinion says the plant was not seen.

## Meetings are not floor time

A kickoff, a management presentation, and a lunch in which the architecture is explained do not count as hours on the floor. Write them separately. If the day is six hours and five of them are slides, you had one hour. The opinion may not sound like a day on site. It sounds like one hour, plus the documents you had already read. That is more useful to a lender than a warm account of a hosted visit.

Ask at the start of the day for the floor time in the calendar, not at the end when the car is waiting. If they will not put it in, you still go to the floor and you write that the schedule did not include it. A schedule that did not include the floor is a finding about the visit, when the engagement was to see the plant.

## The master TDD scorecard and financing opinion governance

A technical opinion rendered to an institutional lender or infrastructure buyer is not an informal technical narrative. It is an auditable legal instrument that directly underwrites syndicated debt or equity investment:

![Figure 09.7: Master Technical Due Diligence Scorecard and Financing Opinion Ledger](../figures/fig-07-master-tdd-scorecard-and-refusal-matrix.png)

The assessment is anchored on Six Critical Pillars:
1. **Civil & Site**: FEMA 500-year Base Flood Elevation compliance (+1.0–1.5 m clearance), seismic SDC lateral restraints, soil subsidence surveys, perimeter blast standoff (≥ 30 m), and clear zoning use permits.
2. **Power & Grid**: True transmission substation physical diversity, uncurtailable firm capacity reservation letters, 72-hour on-site fuel autonomy with duplex polishing, and N+1 concurrently maintainable standby generation.
3. **Cooling & Water**: Concurrently maintainable chiller plant (N+1 / 2N), 48-hour on-site makeup water storage, stratified chilled water thermal energy storage buffer (TES) covering 7–12 min chiller restart latency, and closed-loop liquid CDU readiness.
4. **Structural & Space**: Floor live load rating ≥ 20 kN/m² for dense liquid-cooled AI racks, concentrated caster point loading ≥ 15 kN, heavy freight elevator capacity ≥ 3,500 kg, and GPR-scanned slab coring approval.
5. **Permitting & Legal**: Final unconditional Certificate of Occupancy, Phase I/II Environmental Site Assessment clearance, certified local fire authority sign-off, and clean utility easement rights-of-way.
6. **Operations & Spares**: Manned 24/7/365 multi-disciplinary shift roster, OEM service level agreements with 4-hour response, verified MOP/SOP library, and critical emergency spares stocked on site.

The financing outcome follows a strict four-level governance ladder:
- **Level 1: Unconditional Pass (Clean Opinion)**: All six pillars fully evidenced against primary records. Full dual feeds and unencumbered title proven. 100% of Conditions Precedent satisfied. Debt syndication authorized.
- **Level 2: Conditional Close (Escrow Holdback)**: Tier 2 material deficiencies identified (e.g. missing fuel polishing, punchlist commissioning items). Loan closes subject to mandatory Conditions Precedent and 150%–200% CAPEX escrow holdbacks.
- **Level 3: Material Adverse Finding (Deal Re-Trade)**: Certified technical capacity derated (e.g. power grid capped at 15 MW rather than 20 MW due to thermal line sag). Purchase price or debt commitment renegotiated downward.
- **Level 4: Formal Technical Refusal (Fatal Flaw)**: Uninsurable flood exposure, lack of firm utility capacity reservation, unpermitted whitespace, or denial of physical site inspection. Technical opinion formally withheld.

## Site hydrology, flood plain elevations, and seismic envelopes

A facility that passes a curb tour may harbor catastrophic civil and environmental liabilities. The TDD audit must reconcile the physical site against certified civil hydrology and geotechnical surveys:

![Figure 09.3: Natural Hazard Hydrology, 500-Year Flood Elevation and Seismic Design](../figures/fig-03-natural-hazards-and-flood-plain-hydrology.png)

### 1. Civil hydrology and the 500-year Base Flood Elevation (BFE)

Commercial building codes often cite the 100-year flood plain (1.0% annual exceedance probability) as the regulatory baseline. For mission-critical data centers underwriting hundreds of millions of dollars in syndicated debt, the 100-year benchmark is completely unacceptable.

**The Golden Rule of Data Center Elevation**:
Under GB 50174 Class A and institutional infrastructure underwriting guidelines, all critical IT whitespace Finished Floor Levels (FFL) and essential mechanical/electrical plinths must be elevated at least 1.0 m–1.5 m above the certified **500-Year Base Flood Elevation (0.2% annual exceedance probability)**.

Furthermore, on-site stormwater management must be engineered to retain a 100-year, 24-hour storm event without relying on municipal storm sewers. Peak stormwater runoff rate (Q) is governed by the Rational Method:

$$Q = C \times I \times A$$

Where:
- Q is peak runoff rate (m³/s).
- C is the dimensionless runoff coefficient (0.85–0.95 for paved data center roofs, roadways, and transformer yards).
- I is rainfall intensity (mm/hr) for the 100-year design storm.
- A is total contributing catchment area (m²).

The auditor must inspect the on-site retention basin, weir discharge invert elevations, and backup diesel-driven stormwater sump pumps. If the site relies on gravity storm discharge into an off-site canal whose crest elevation exceeds the facility yard during storm surges, log a critical Tier 1 flood risk.

Sub-grade basements must be audited for hydrostatic uplift. Where the historical high water table rises above the basement slab elevation, verify dual-layer bentonite or elastomeric waterproofing membranes and continuous dewatering sump pits with emergency generator power backing.

### 2. Seismic design category and lateral component bracing

Under IBC / ASCE 7-22 and GB 50011, data centers are classified as Essential Facilities with a Component Importance Factor I_p = 1.5. In Seismic Design Categories (SDC) D, E, or F, or where Peak Ground Acceleration (PGA) exceeds 0.25g:
- Medium-voltage transformers, centrifugal chillers, cooling towers, and standby diesel generators must be anchored with certified seismic snubbers to prevent shear dislocation.
- Raised access floor pedestals must feature welded diagonal seismic strut bracing tied directly into the concrete structural subfloor.
- VRLA and lithium-ion battery racks must incorporate heavy-gauge steel side retainers and floor anchor plates tested to withstand horizontal lateral acceleration F_p = 0.4 · S_DS · I_p · W_p.

### 3. Perimeter hazard exclusion buffers

A technical due diligence site audit must verify mandatory geographical separation distances:
- **Commercial Airport Runways**: Minimum 8.0 km from the centerline of active commercial jet runways to avoid FAA Part 77 imaginary airspace penetration and high-frequency acoustic/vibration coupling.
- **Toxic / Chemical Hazmat Facilities**: Minimum 1.5 km buffer from chemical refineries, fertilizer storage, and toxic gas facilities to eliminate plume corrosion (H_2S, SO_2) or toxic evacuation zones.
- **Heavy Freight Rail Corridors**: Minimum 150 m setback to prevent low-frequency ground vibration transmission (<10 Hz) from inducing head crashes in rotating media or optical switch transceiver jitter.
- **Overhead High-Voltage Lines**: No high-voltage transmission lines (>110 kV) may traverse directly above the data hall whitespace footprint.

# 22. Obsolescence is a contract fact

You will be asked whether the equipment will still be the equipment the revenue assumes, a few years out. That question has a technical part and a market part. You do not answer the market part.

The technical part is short. What generation does the contract assume. Is that what you counted, on the sample, against the asset record. Does a supply document say what happens if that generation cannot be repaired or replaced. Is there a spare path for the failures you can expect in ordinary operation, evidenced on a shelf or only in a sentence. Lifespan in a brochure is not a remaining life. Depreciation in a model is not a remaining life. You may quote a document that states a manufacturer's figure, and you say you did not test it.

You do not compare the generation to whatever is newest, and you do not rank the site against other regions. A newer card existing in the world is not a finding that this contract fails. ## Structural floor loading, concentrated loads, and AI rack density

When lenders or buyers ask whether a facility can support "next-generation workloads," they are rarely asking about silicon instruction sets. They are asking whether the physical civil and structural envelope can bear the extreme mechanical mass of modern AI accelerators:

![Figure 09.6: Structural Floor Loading, Concentrated Loads and AI Accelerator Weight](../figures/fig-06-structural-floor-loading-and-expansion-limits.png)

### 1. Floor Uniformly Distributed Load (UDL) spectrum

Data centers engineered for traditional enterprise servers cannot physically support high-density liquid-cooled AI clusters:

| Equipment Tier | Uniformly Distributed Load (UDL) | Area Mass Equivalent | Typical Rack Thermal Envelope |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Legacy Enterprise** | 5.0–8.0 kN/m² | 500–800 kg/m² | 5–10 kW Air-Cooled |
| **Hyperscale Cloud** | 10.0–12.0 kN/m² | 1,000–1,200 kg/m² | 15–20 kW Air-Cooled |
| **AI Supercluster (NVL72)** | **18.0–25.0 kN/m²** | **1,800–2,500 kg/m²** | **120–140 kW Liquid-Cooled** |
| **Central Plant / Battery** | 25.0–35.0 kN/m² | 2,500–3,500 kg/m² | Heavy Mechanical / VRLA |

An NVL72 liquid-cooled rack footprint weighs approximately 1,360 kg dry and up to 1,800 kg operational (including manifolds, quick-disconnect hoses, internal blind-mate fluid loops, and power busbars). Under GB 50174 Class A guidelines, deploying an AI supercluster onto a slab rated for only 10–12 kN/m² creates immediate structural deflection, cracking, and eventual shear failure.

### 2. Concentrated caster point loading and pedestal stress

During roll-in and positioning, the entire static and dynamic mass of an 1,800 kg NVL72 rack rests on four caster wheels:

$$P_{\text{point}} = \frac{M_{\text{rack}} \times g}{4} = \frac{1,800\,\text{kg} \times 9.807\,\text{m/s}^2}{4} \approx 4.41\,\text{kN per caster}$$

When rolled across a raised floor, dynamic shock coefficients increase this point load to > 6.5 kN. 
The TDD auditor must review the certified raised access floor test certificates (CISCA / MOB PSA):
- **Concentrated Load Rating**: Must be certified to ≥ 15.0 kN per pedestal without exceeding L/360 deflection.
- **Rolling Load Rating**: Must be certified to ≥ 10.0 kN under 10,000 passes to prevent surface panel punch-through during rack transit.

### 3. Post-tensioned (PT) slab coring: the liquid retrofit trap

In facilities undergoing retrofit from air cooling to liquid cooling, large-diameter supply and return coolant pipes (DN100–DN200) must penetrate floor slabs to reach the sub-floor Coolant Distribution Unit (CDU) or chiller headers.

In post-tensioned (PT) concrete buildings, drilling floor penetrations without structural radar scans can sever high-tensile steel tensioning tendons. Severing a single post-tensioned tendon releases hundreds of kilonewtons of stored kinetic energy, causing catastrophic slab failure across multiple bays.

**The TDD Audit Rule**:
Demand certified Ground Penetrating Radar (GPR) slab scan reports and a structural engineer's stamped core penetration plan for all existing and planned liquid cooling piping penetrations. If penetrations were cored without GPR verification, log a mandatory Tier 1 structural safety finding.

### 4. Rigging pathways and freight logistics

Physical accessibility is a frequent hidden bottleneck:
- **Freight Elevator Payload**: Must be certified to ≥ 3,500 kg (3.5 tons) with clear internal cab dimensions of at least 2.4 m (W) × 2.4 m (H) × 3.0 m (D) to transport crated high-density racks.
- **Loading Dock Ramp Slope**: Maximum allowable incline is ≤ 5% (1:20). Ramps steeper than 5% create extreme rollover hazards for racks with a high center of gravity (H_cog ≥ 1.1 m).
- **Corridor Turning Radii**: Clear double-door openings (≥ 1.5 m) and flat transitions with zero unramped thresholds.

# 24. Do not smooth a contradiction

# 27. How to read a power claim

They will show a single line, a capacity number, and a sentence about redundancy. You are not redrawing the line. You are testing whether the claim and the evidence are the same width.

Start with the claim, not the drawing. What must stay up for the performance they sell. The equipment in scope, or the whole building, or a named suite. If the claim does not say, the drawing cannot save it. A drawing of the whole site does not prove the suite, and a drawing of the suite does not prove the site.

Then the path. Two feeds, a generator, a battery, a second board: each is a name until a document says it was tested, or you saw the test. A brochure runtime is not the test. A single-line note that says N+1 is not the test. Spoken redundancy is not the test. Write tested, documented, or spoken. Only the first two may enter the opinion, and documented means you opened the record, not that they mentioned it.

Then the number. Design kilowatts, nameplate, and a measured load are three numbers. Do not add them. Do not put design in the operating cell because the meter was inconvenient. If the financing model uses a load the visit cannot see, say the model’s load is not your finding. Counsel and the model owner own that cell. You own whether a live point exists and whether you were shown it.

A utility application, a transformer rating, and an expansion still on paper are future path, not present path. Label future. A future path does not support a sentence about tonight’s equipment. If the equipment in scope is already installed, the path that feeds it now is the one that matters. The expansion may be a separate sentence, evidenced or not.

You do not copy the drawing into the report. You cite the revision you read, and you say whether you accept it as current. Floor and drawing disagree: neither supports a precise claim until they agree. You can still support a coarser one: a second path was not shown.

## Utility substation diversity: physical reality versus marketing claim

The most dangerous failure in data center due diligence is accepting a sales brochure's claim of "dual utility feeds" without auditing the physical underground routing:

![Figure 09.2: Utility Substation Diversity and Grid Dual-Feed Reality](../figures/fig-02-utility-substation-and-grid-diversity.png)

### 1. The pseudo-diverse single-bus trap

Developers frequently present two separate utility power bills or two meter accounts as evidence of redundancy. In reality, both medium-voltage feeders often originate from the same primary utility transmission substation, connected to the same high-voltage transformer bus section. 

Even worse, both medium-voltage cables are frequently pulled through the exact same underground duct bank or street utility manhole outside the campus gate. A single municipal excavator strike, vehicle collision with a riser pole, or manhole cable fire severs both feeds simultaneously, collapsing the entire data center onto standby generators.

### 2. True physical transmission grid diversity

Under Uptime Tier III / IV and GB 50174 Class A criteria, true electrical grid diversity requires:
- **Independent Transmission Substations**: Feeder A and Feeder B must originate from two distinct, geographically separate utility transmission substations (e.g. Substation North at 220 kV and Substation South at 110 kV), powered by separate grid transmission networks.
- **Geographically Diverse Cable Routing**: The two cable routes must follow independent rights-of-way separated by at least 1.5–2.5 km, with zero shared utility trenches, duct banks, bridges, or manholes.
- **Dual Campus Entry Vaults**: The two feeders must enter the data center property from opposite street boundaries, through concrete-encased duct banks terminating in independent, blast-separated medium-voltage electrical vaults located at least 20 m apart.

### 3. Electrical TDD audit checklist

When auditing the electrical single-line diagram and utility agreements, verify five hard technical parameters:
1. **Capacity Allocation Letter**: Inspect the utility's formal capacity reservation letter. Verify whether the capacity is **firm and uncurtailable** or subject to seasonal grid curtailment / clawback clauses. A utility application or pending permit is future capacity, not present capacity.
2. **N-1 Emergency Capacity Contingency**: Each utility feeder and primary transformer must be rated to carry 100% of the facility's maximum coincident peak load plus UPS battery recharge current (I_recharge ≈ 15%–20% of UPS rating) continuously under single-path N-1 operation:
 $$S_{\text{feeder}} \ge \frac{P_{\text{facility\_peak}} + P_{\text{recharge}}}{\text{PF}} \times 1.10$$
3. **Breaker Transfer Scheme and Latency**: Audit the medium-voltage bus-tie transfer logic. Is it an Automatic Transfer Switch (ATS) with open-transition break-before-make latency (2–10 seconds), or a closed-transition sync-check scheme? Confirm that UPS energy storage ride-through seamlessly bridges the transfer window without IT power disruption.
4. **Short-Circuit Withstand & Arc-Flash Hazard**: Verify that switchgear busbars and breakers have certified short-circuit withstand ratings (I_sc ≥ 40 kA or 65 kA) conforming to IEEE Std 141 short-circuit study results. Arc-flash warning labels must display incident energy levels (cal/cm²) dated within the last three years.
5. **Power Factor & Harmonic Distortion**: Reconcile utility revenue meters against facility BMS power meters. Verify power factor PF ≥ 0.95 and total harmonic voltage distortion THD_v ≤ 5.0% (IEEE 519) to prevent utility tariff surcharges or filter resonance trips.

## Decisions to record

- N-1 single-feeder emergency capacity verified with battery recharge load.

# 30. One visit, as a method

This chapter walks one visit so the sheet has a shape. Nothing in it is a site. There is no address, no party, no serial, and no load. Where a number would have been, the text says "the performance sentence" or "the sample you wrote."

The reader is a lender. The engagement asks you to say whether the operator can deliver the equipment that was bought, in a room someone else runs, to a customer who specified it. It does not ask you to audit the customer's jobs. You write that line in the first paragraph you will ever send, before you describe a room.

## Before

The data room opens late. You list the unread documents before you fly. You do not treat the flight as a review of them. On the plane you have their performance sentence, copied, and a sample rule: identity on a written fraction of rows, running state on a written few machines, not a feeling. You do not bring a failure-rate formula. You do not bring last year's findings.

## There

Three chairs. The room provider owes power and cooling. The operator owes the equipment, the spares, and the night ticket. The customer owes the specification and their own acceptance test. A service level in the customer contract assumes all three. You do not let the operator's confidence clear the room provider's untested second feed.

You count against their list. The totals meet. You do not publish the list. The identity sample matches, except one function the record calls present and you do not find. That is a finding, five lines, not a story about a busy dock. The running sample includes a dark machine. You write dark. You do not average it into the row.

Their platform test is a document from the week before, signed, scope narrower than the contract title. You accept it as a document for that scope. You do not witness it. You do not let the title widen your verb. A demonstration they offer in the afternoon, outside the script, is refused as a test.

The person who would be called cannot say the first safe state. The binder has an emergency paper of many pages. You find that the paper is not issued. You do not grade the person. The spare list is long. The part the job will not start without is not on the shelf. List only, not checked. That is the sentence.

Power is a drawing you accept as current enough to see two paths drawn, and a test record for one of them. The second is spoken. Spoken does not enter the opinion as tested. Design kilowatts stay out of the cell.

## The opinion

You sign that the count reconciled, that the identity sample matched except the numbered finding, that one test document exists for a narrower scope, and that you did not audit the customer's system. You do not sign that the performance level is achieved. The chair map is incomplete where the second power path is only a speech, and the spare is only a list. Those limits sit in the same section as the yes. A market paragraph is not in the draft. A probability is not in the draft.

They ask you to delete the dark machine because it was "about to be fixed." You do not. They send a document the next week. You read it. You close that number or you do not. You do not reissue a broad no-issue.

They also ask whether the model load is fair. The model uses a design figure. You tag it as design and do not replace it with a number of your own. Insurance adequacy is not asked in a way you can answer. You do not answer it. Chapter 32. The door sentence and the opinion stay the same width. You do not add "overall satisfactory" in the covering mail. The covering mail is part of the opinion if they will forward it. Write it as narrowly as the sheet.

## What this visit was for

You leave with their sentence copied, your sentence narrower, findings numbered, rooms you did not enter listed, and no site a later reader can name. That is a due diligence. A thicker report that hides the speech and the dark machine is not.

# 31. Conditions are not compliments

Financing documents turn your findings into conditions: things that must be true before money moves, or before a date. You do not draft their conditions. You do say which findings can be closed by a document, which need a later look, and which cannot be closed by a promise.

A missing paper can be a condition with a date. The date is theirs and counsel's. Your part is what the paper must contain, in one line: the revision, the signature, the scope. "Provide comfort" is not a line. A paper that arrives and does not contain the line does not close the condition. You say so. You do not negotiate the date.

A dark machine, a spare not on the shelf, a second path only spoken, cannot be closed by a letter that says they will be handled. Those need a look, or a document that is itself a record of a test or a delivery, not an intention. Intention is the questionnaire. You already had the questionnaire.

Do not let a condition be written so widely that closing it requires you to audit the layer you refused. If they draft "technical due diligence satisfied in all respects," strike it. Your opinion was never all respects. The condition, if they need one, cites the numbered findings still open, not your mood about the project.

A condition you cannot imagine evidencing is not a useful condition. Say that. A flood study you are not competent to sign should not be a condition that waits on your signature. It waits on the person whose job that is. Chapter 18. Splitting the names is part of the work, even after the report has gone.

# 32. Cost, insurance, and models are not the opinion

A data room will contain an operating-cost sheet, an insurance summary, and a financial model that uses a load, an efficiency, and a life. You will be asked if the technical inputs are fair. Fair is a wide word. Answer the narrow one.

A load in the model is fair only if you can point to the evidence: measured and live, or a contract quantity you read, or absent. Design kilowatts are not fair by default. If the model uses design, say it uses design, and that you did not adopt it as the operating case. You do not propose a better number to be helpful. A number you invent in the margin becomes the technical case. Leave the cell as they built it, and tag it.

An efficiency, a loss, a utilization, is fair only if the basis is a document you opened or a meter you were shown. A ratio from a brochure is a brochure. Do not improve it. Do not attack it with a rule of thumb. Rule of thumb is the industry-practice word again.

Insurance is a contract about what is covered after a loss. You do not say the cover is enough. You may say whether the technical description in the proposal matches the plant you saw: the equipment class, the location class, the redundancy they described to the insurer. A mismatch is a finding if the financing relies on that cover existing as described. Adequacy of the sum is not your sentence.

Life in the model is the generation question, chapter 22. Do not let a depreciation schedule become a maintenance finding, or the reverse. They can both be written. They are not the same fact.

If they ask you to sensitize the model, refuse. Sensitivity is their case. Your case is which inputs you will stand behind, and which you will not. A table of your alternative loads is a second model with your name on it. Do not build it.

# 34. How to read a cooling claim

Cooling claims fail in the same place power claims fail: a drawing is treated as a test, and a design condition is treated as tonight. Start with the sentence. What must the room do for the equipment in scope. A supply temperature, a redundancy of units, a water source, a rejection path. If the sentence is only "cooled to industry standard," you do not have a sentence. Industry standard is not a number you may reconstruct. Ask them to point to the contract line. If they cannot, the cooling performance is not stated.

A unit count on a drawing is a name. It is tested only if a document says the remaining units carried the load, or you saw that test. You will not stage that test. Spoken N+1 does not enter the opinion as tested. A control system that rotates units is not a test of what happens when one is off. Rotation is a schedule. Failure is a different hour.

Water is a duration question and a path question. Duration: when the utility stops, is there a volume tied to a demand in a document, or only a tank you saw. A tank without the tie is a tank. Path: one source or two, and whether the second is real. A loop drawn on paper is not a second source until a document or a valve you can have explained shows it can feed the plant alone. Do not estimate liters. Do not adopt their daily use without the basis.

Heat rejection outside, towers or dry coolers or whatever they use, is in scope only as far as the contract's cooling sentence needs it. You are not optimizing it. A model of fan speed is not a finding that the plant is efficient. Efficiency was not the question unless the engagement asked, and even then only a meter you were shown can support a number. Chapter 32. A tower that is running is not a test of approach. Approach is a measurement with a clock, or it is not in the opinion.

Leaks that can reach the equipment are the risk you can see without becoming the designer. Route evidenced, route not entered, or route only described. Description is an interview. The floor void you did not enter is not dry.

## The cooling audit formula and thermal ride-through

As detailed in Figure 09.4, an evaporative cooling claim must be reconciled against the thermodynamic water demand:
- **Evaporative Consumption**: Wet cooling towers consume approximately 1.5–2.2 liters per kilowatt-hour of heat rejected. For a 20 MW continuous critical heat load, the 48-hour emergency water reserve requires:
 $$V_{\text{water}} = 20,000\,\text{kW} \times 1.8\,\text{L/kWh} \times 48\,\text{hours} = 1,728,000\,\text{liters} \quad (\approx 1,728\,\text{m}^3)$$
 If the on-site raw water storage tanks fall below this volume and dual independent municipal water mains are not proven via physical utility GIS route maps, log an emergency water duration deficiency.
- **Chilled Water Thermal Energy Storage (TES)**: Centrifugal chillers require 7–12 minutes (t_restart) to restart following an electrical transfer. In high-density AI whitespaces, coolant supply temperature spikes beyond IT warranty limits within 90 seconds unless an inline stratified thermal buffer is installed:
 $$V_{\text{tes}} = \frac{Q_{\text{cooling}} \times t_{\text{restart}}}{\rho \times C_p \times \Delta T}$$
 Verify that the secondary loop thermal inertia or TES tank volume satisfies this equation.
- **Closed-Loop Liquid Secondary Circuit**: For direct-to-chip liquid cooling loops (CDUs), inspect water quality logs: electrical conductivity must be maintained at ≤ 10–30 μS/cm (or ≤ 100 μS/cm with propylene glycol), and biocide reserves must be evidenced.

- CDU closed-loop fluid conductivity and corrosion inhibition records verified.

# 35. The early flag list is not the opinion

The engagement may ask for flags before the full report, because money has a timetable. A flag list is a narrower product. It says what you already know that would change the financing sentence. It does not say the visit is finished. It does not say there are no other findings. If you cannot write that limit in the first lines, do not send the list. A list without the limit will be forwarded as the opinion.

What may be a flag: a performance sentence with no evidence in the papers you have. A chair that cannot be named. An identity sample already failed. A power or cooling path that is only a speech, where the contract needs it tested. A permit counsel said they need, which you were not shown. A spare the job refuses to start without, absent, where the service level assumes replacement. What may not be a flag: a cable you dislike, a brand you would not have chosen, a market worry, a procedure you have not yet sampled. Those wait for the report, or they are not findings at all.

Number the flags. Date them. Say what would close each one. A flag you cannot close with a document or a look is a flag about the deal, not a homework item. Say that, so nobody assigns it to you as a punch line you must clear by Friday.

Do not upgrade the list when they ask if there is "anything else" on a call. Anything else is the unread room and the unwalked floor. Say unread and unwalked. Do not invent a second flag to sound careful, and do not clear a flag on the call because they explained it. Explanation is an interview. The flag stays until the tag changes to a document you have read or a thing you have seen.

The full opinion later may add findings. It may not silently drop a flag because the tone of the final report is calmer. If a flag is closed, close it by number, with the evidence. If it is still open, it appears again. A calm executive summary that omits an open flag is a false document.

A license that covers the use. Each property is evidenced by a document you opened or a thing you saw, or it is not in the opinion. A diagram with two colors is not yet a redundant path. A diagram is a claim. The claim needs the same tags as a power path: tested, documented, or spoken.

You do not copy the port list, the addressing plan, or the configuration. Those are identifiers and they go stale the week they renumber a device. Your sentence says the sample of devices matched the asset record, or did not, by function. Role names are enough. Their hostnames are not.

Do not score the architecture against one you prefer. Preference is not obsolescence, and it is not a finding. A finding is a mismatch with their contract, or a path that is only a speech where they need it to carry the performance. Another cluster you have seen, with a different fabric, is another hall. It does not set the bar.

If the tenant operates the network and your line stops under their system, you stop at identity of the boxes the operator delivered. How those boxes are configured tonight may be the tenant's chair. Say so. Do not configure-check your way across the line because the screens were open. Open screens are how scope creeps into a false network audit.

A single sample of "it pinged" is not a performance test. It is a moment. If they have a test document for the network, chapter 23 applies. Witness, accept the document, or not shown. A ping you asked for is the staged demonstration. Refuse it as the performance.

4. **Zone 4: Whitespace Airlock Mantraps**:

### 3. Physical security audit field checks

During the physical walk, the auditor must execute three active field verifications:

- **Door Forced Alarm Test**: Test a designated emergency exit door. Confirm that an unauthorized door-opening generates an immediate audible/visual alarm at the SOC workstation and triggers automatic CCTV camera preset call-up in under 3 seconds.

# 38. Two files, not one notebook

You will accumulate more than the opinion can hold. Split it the day you start, or their identifiers will leak into the thing you send.

"The person on the roster for that hour" is the role. Their name is not the finding.

# 39. A buyer and a lender do not get the same extra

The sheet is the same. The temptation is not. A lender asks whether the assumption in the financing is evidenced, and what would break it. A buyer asks whether they will inherit a plant they can live with. Both questions can be answered from the same look. They cannot be answered by adding the sentences the other one wanted.

For a lender, do not become the advocate. Do not add comfort because the timetable is real. A missing second path is a missing second path on the day money moves, not a detail to be solved in operations later. Later is a condition, chapter 31, or it is a limit in the opinion. It is not a soft yes.

For a buyer, do not become the operator. They will ask what you would fix first, what you would budget, what you would renegotiate. The first of those can be a numbered finding already in the opinion. The budget and the negotiation are not your visit. A list of projects you would run is a design. You were not engaged to design. If they want that, it is a different paper, after they own it, with a different sheet.

Do not keep two opinions in one report, one cautious for the lender and one warmer for the sponsor who paid the invoice. One look, one set of findings, one sentence. The reader is named in the engagement. If both will read it, the sentence is the narrower one, and you say both are readers. A warmer annex for the sponsor is a second opinion. Second opinions in the same week are how the careful one gets discarded.

Your duty runs to the sentence you signed, not to the party who was kindest on site. Kindness is not evidence. Access that depended on kindness is a limit if it can be withdrawn. Write the access you actually had. Chapter 26. A visit that saw only what a host selected is a selected visit. The opinion says selected, or it pretends to have seen the plant.

If the sponsor paid the invoice and the lender is the reader, the sponsor does not get a private softening. Payment is not a second engagement. Say that when the invoice is sent, not when the draft comes back with two sets of comments. Two sets of comments are answered in one sheet. The narrower sentence wins. A finding deleted for the sponsor and kept for the lender is two opinions. You do not keep two.
