# 0. How to use this book

This book is for the person sent to a data center to say, in writing, what a lender or a buyer may rely on. The visit is a technical due diligence. It is not a design. It is not a commissioning. It is not an audit of the tenant's applications.

The method comes from a lender's technical visit on a live hall: desk review, a questionnaire, interviews, a walk, a count, a sample, and a witness of tests someone else ran. The site, the parties, the addresses, and the equipment list stay out. If a sentence here starts to look like that report, it does not belong.

The earlier books already say when a file is a pass, when a screen may speak, and when a receiver refuses a signature. This book is the visit that produces the sentence those people will be asked to trust. You do not rerun their heat window. You do not sign their operation. You write what you saw, what you accepted as a document, and what you did not look at.

When you finish, you should be able to hand over one visit sheet. It names the scope, the chairs, the sample rule, and the sentence you will sign. It is in chapter 14. If you fly tomorrow, start there. The week before is chapter 26. A visit with no site, so the sheet has a shape, is chapter 30. Conditions someone will draft from your findings are chapter 31.

## What you may copy

You may copy the sheet headings. You may not paste a sentence from this book into a customer's report and call it their finding. Their contract, their drawings, and their serial lists stay in their file.

## Decisions to record

- The sentence the engagement letter already asks you to sign.
- The layer you will not audit.
- The sample rule, written before you land.
