# 35. The early flag list is not the opinion

The engagement may ask for flags before the full report, because money has a timetable. A flag list is a narrower product. It says what you already know that would change the financing sentence. It does not say the visit is finished. It does not say there are no other findings. If you cannot write that limit in the first lines, do not send the list. A list without the limit will be forwarded as the opinion.

What may be a flag: a performance sentence with no evidence in the papers you have. A chair that cannot be named. An identity sample already failed. A power or cooling path that is only a speech, where the contract needs it tested. A permit counsel said they need, which you were not shown. A spare the job refuses to start without, absent, where the service level assumes replacement. What may not be a flag: a cable you dislike, a brand you would not have chosen, a market worry, a procedure you have not yet sampled. Those wait for the report, or they are not findings at all.

Number the flags. Date them. Say what would close each one. A flag you cannot close with a document or a look is a flag about the deal, not a homework item. Say that, so nobody assigns it to you as a punch line you must clear by Friday.

Do not upgrade the list when they ask if there is "anything else" on a call. Anything else is the unread room and the unwalked floor. Say unread and unwalked. Do not invent a second flag to sound careful, and do not clear a flag on the call because they explained it. Explanation is an interview. The flag stays until the tag changes to a document you have read or a thing you have seen.

The full opinion later may add findings. It may not silently drop a flag because the tone of the final report is calmer. If a flag is closed, close it by number, with the evidence. If it is still open, it appears again. A calm executive summary that omits an open flag is a false document.

## Interim red-flag reporting and financing covenants

As structured in Figures 09.1 and 09.7, the Interim Red-Flag Report is delivered within 48 hours of completing the physical site walk:

1. **Mandatory Header Disclaimers**: Every interim red-flag memorandum must state prominently on page 1:
 > *"This Interim Red-Flag Report reflects preliminary technical due diligence findings based solely on documents reviewed and physical assets inspected as of [Date]. It does not constitute a final engineering opinion, does not represent a complete audit of all facility systems, and cannot be relied upon for loan syndication or financial closing."*
2. **The Three-Tier Covenant Governance**:
 - **Tier 1: Fatal Flaws (Red)**: Deal-breaking deficiencies that halt loan closing or trigger contract termination rights (e.g. active utility capacity clawbacks, uninsurable flood plain siting, missing occupancy permits).
 - **Tier 2: Material Deficiencies / Conditions Precedent (Amber)**: Required physical rectifications or documentation that must be fully closed prior to initial debt drawdown (e.g. installation of duplex fuel polishing, testing of emergency ATS transfer, replacement of degraded batteries). Where remediation cannot be completed before closing, require a **150%–200% financial escrow holdback** of the estimated CAPEX remediation cost, locked until the independent engineer signs off on the cure.
 - **Tier 3: Operational Defects / Conditions Subsequent (Green)**: Low-risk items that the borrower is legally covenanted to cure within 60 to 90 days following financial close (e.g. updating as-built redlines, completing minor spare parts inventory replenishment).
3. **Closing a Flag**: A flag can only be closed by a verifiable evidentiary artifact: an executed utility agreement, an stamped structural engineering calculation, a certified laboratory test report, or a re-inspection photo. Verbal assurances in a conference call do not close a flag.

## Decisions to record

- Flag list labeled not the opinion, in the first lines.
- Each flag categorized into Tier 1 (Fatal Flaw), Tier 2 (Condition Precedent / Escrow), or Tier 3 (Condition Subsequent).
- Each flag: what would close it, document or look.
- A call that tried to clear a flag verbally, refused.
- Open flags repeated in the final opinion, by number.
