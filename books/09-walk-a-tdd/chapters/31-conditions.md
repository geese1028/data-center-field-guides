# 31. Conditions are not compliments

Financing documents turn your findings into conditions: things that must be true before money moves, or before a date. You do not draft their conditions. You do say which findings can be closed by a document, which need a later look, and which cannot be closed by a promise.

A missing paper can be a condition with a date. The date is theirs and counsel's. Your part is what the paper must contain, in one line: the revision, the signature, the scope. "Provide comfort" is not a line. A paper that arrives and does not contain the line does not close the condition. You say so. You do not negotiate the date.

A dark machine, a spare not on the shelf, a second path only spoken, cannot be closed by a letter that says they will be handled. Those need a look, or a document that is itself a record of a test or a delivery, not an intention. Intention is the questionnaire. You already had the questionnaire.

Do not let a condition be written so widely that closing it requires you to audit the layer you refused. If they draft "technical due diligence satisfied in all respects," strike it. Your opinion was never all respects. The condition, if they need one, cites the numbered findings still open, not your mood about the project.

A condition you cannot imagine evidencing is not a useful condition. Say that. A flood study you are not competent to sign should not be a condition that waits on your signature. It waits on the person whose job that is. Chapter 18. Splitting the names is part of the work, even after the report has gone.

## Decisions to record

- For each open finding: document can close it, or only a later look.
- A condition drafted as "all respects," struck.
- Intentions not accepted as closes.
- A condition that is not yours, named as not yours.
