# 19. A finding is a change to their sentence

A note that the hall is interesting is not a finding. A finding changes a sentence they are relying on: the performance can be met, the equipment is what was bought, the chair who owes the night can be named, the path was tested. If it does not change one of those, it goes in an appendix of observations or it does not go in at all. Lenders will read the first page. Do not hide a real finding under observations, and do not promote an observation to scare a timetable.

Each finding has five lines. What you expected, from their contract or their own questionnaire. What you found. The tag: seen, document, or interview. Which chair owes the next act. What sentence you will not sign until it is closed. A finding without the fifth line is a complaint. Complaints are not opinions.

Close is their work, evidenced, not your rewrite. They may show a document you then read. They may walk you back to a room you record as seen on the return, not on the day you wrote the finding. A verbal assurance in the comment column does not close it. The verification book already refused that close. So does this one.

"No issue" on a layer is a finding of a sort, and it needs the look. No issue on the sample. No issue in the documents opened. No issue is not a property of the project. When you write it, write the boundary in the same line, or a reader will extend it to the layer you refused to audit.

## Decisions to record

- Findings that change a relied-on sentence, five lines each.
- Observations that do not, kept out of the opinion or marked as observations.
- Closes that were documents or a later look. Verbal closes refused.
- Every no-issue line carrying its boundary.
