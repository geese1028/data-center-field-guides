# 18. What you hand to counsel

Some questions in a financing look like technical questions and are not yours to close. Permits and authorizations: you may list what you were shown, and whether the cover matches the asset the engagement names. You do not decide that the authority was the right authority, or that the filing is sufficient. That sentence is counsel's. If they ask you to confirm it, you confirm only that the document was in the room and that you are not opining. A joint sentence that hides your limit is the wide sentence again.

Warranties and revenue shortfall sit on the same line. Counsel reads whether the supply agreement pays if the equipment cannot do what was sold. You read whether the technical assumption is evidenced: the equipment is the equipment, the performance sentence exists, the test verb is honest. You do not compute the shortfall. You do not say the warranty is adequate. Adequate is their word. Evidenced is yours.

A license is a technical-looking commercial fact. Ask whether the delivery includes software rights the operation needs, and whether a document says those rights were bought for this use. You do not interpret the license. You write "document names this use" or "not shown." An operator who says the rights are full, with no document, is an interview. Interviews are not licenses.

Do not let counsel's draft of your opinion add a legal comfort you did not earn, and do not let your draft add a permit conclusion they did not write. Two sections. Cross-reference. Do not merge.

## Decisions to record

- Permits listed as seen. Sufficiency left to counsel.
- Warranty adequacy not signed. Technical assumption evidenced or not.
- License: document, interview, or not shown.
- Merged legal-technical paragraph refused.
