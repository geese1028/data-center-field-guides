# 16. Titles, and the copyright line

This book teaches a visit sheet for a lender's or a buyer's technical due diligence. It does not digest a financing agreement, a customer's report, or a standard.

The method is the one used when the window was short and the scope stopped under the tenant's system: read the engagement first, separate the chairs, ask before you walk, count and sample under a rule written in advance, witness or accept a document and do not merge the verbs, refuse a probability in place of a spare, and put the limits in the same section as the opinion. The hall is not in this book.

A design standard, a tier label, or a facilities title may be named when the contract already names it. Do not paste it. ASHRAE titles are under the same rule. ASHRAE has said its publication content is not to be entered into an AI tool, and that AI derivative works of that content need written permission. This book does not enter that content, and it does not carry a limit out of a slide.

A commissioning certificate set may already be how the project names levels. The third book keeps the numberings apart. This visit reads the map. It does not issue the certificates.

If a sentence here starts to look like a finding from a report you actually issued, cut it. Leave the decision.

A questionnaire from another engagement, a sample percentage from a previous visit, and a market paragraph from a slide are the same kind of paste as a serial list. They do not become this visit because they were useful once. Write this visit's rule. Leave the old one in the old file.

## Technical standards and reference authorities

When formulating technical findings, reference recognized national and international engineering consensus standards by formal title and edition, avoiding informal industry folklore:

### 1. Due diligence and property condition assessment
- **ASTM E2018-24**: *Standard Guide for Property Condition Assessments: Baseline Property Condition Assessment Process*. Defines professional walk methodology, physical deficiency identification, remaining useful life (RUL) estimation, and baseline capital expenditure (CAPEX) replacement reserves.

### 2. Civil, structural, and site hydrology
- **FEMA Flood Insurance Study (FIS) Guidelines & 44 CFR Part 60**: Flood elevation definitions, including the 100-year Base Flood Elevation (1.0% annual exceedance probability) and 500-year Base Flood Elevation (0.2% annual exceedance probability).
- **GB 50174-2017**: *数据中心设计规范 (Code for Design of Data Centers)*. Mandates Class A/B/C facility classification, finished floor elevation requirements above historical flood levels, floor live loading envelopes (10–25 kN/m²), and electromagnetic shielding.
- **GB 50011-2010 (2016 Edition)**: *建筑抗震设计规范 (Code for Seismic Design of Buildings)* and **IBC 2024 / ASCE 7-22**: Structural seismic design categories (SDC A through F), peak ground acceleration (PGA), and non-structural component seismic bracing (I_p = 1.5).
- **CISCA / MOB PSA**: *Ceilings & Interior Systems Construction Association: Recommended Test Procedures for Access Floors*. Concentrated load (15 kN), rolling load, and ultimate structural pedestal loading benchmarks.

### 3. Electrical power distribution and standby generation
- **IEEE Std 141-1993 (R1999, IEEE Red Book)**: *IEEE Recommended Practice for Electric Power Distribution for Industrial Plants*. High-voltage substation topologies, short-circuit calculations, and selective coordination.
- **IEEE Std 446-1995 (IEEE Orange Book)**: *IEEE Recommended Practice for Emergency and Standby Power Systems for Industrial and Commercial Applications*. Generator step-load transients, transfer switch topologies, and battery ride-through.
- **ASTM D975-23**: *Standard Specification for Diesel Fuel Oils*.
- **ASTM D2709-22**: *Standard Test Method for Water and Sediment in Middle Distillate Fuels by Centrifuge*. Benchmark limit ≤ 0.05 % vol (50 ppm) for critical diesel engine fuel supply.

### 4. Telecommunications and physical security
- **ANSI/TIA-942-B / TIA-942-C**: *Telecommunications Infrastructure Standard for Data Centers*. Diverse pathways, telecommunications spaces, and dual Meet-Me Room (MMR) architecture.
- **ASTM F2656/F2656M-20**: *Standard Test Method for Crash Testing of Vehicle Security Barriers*. Impact ratings (M30, M40, M50) and penetration limits (P1 ≤ 1.0 m, P2, P3).
- **NFPA 75 & NFPA 76**: *Standard for the Fire Protection of Information Technology Equipment & Telecommunications Facilities*. Very Early Smoke Detection Apparatus (VESDA), clean-agent gaseous extinguishing, and pre-action sprinkler zoning.

## Decisions to record

- Titles cited, edition only.
- The engagement, the asset list, and the real report you did not paste.
- Authoritative consensus standards cited in place of subjective phrases.
