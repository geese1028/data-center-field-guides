# 10. The performance the contract actually names

The technical question is whether they can meet a sentence that is already written. Find that sentence. Availability, a delivery count, a response time, a power or cooling condition. If you cannot find it, you cannot conclude they meet it. You conclude the performance is not stated in the papers you read.

Then map the sentence onto evidence. A service level that assumes the room never drops the equipment needs the room provider's tested path, not the operator's confidence. A level that assumes failed machines are replaced from stock needs the spare you saw, not a mean-time calculation. A level that assumes the customer accepted the platform needs their test document, and your verb: witnessed or accepted as a document.

A shortfall in the contract, revenue, liquidated damages, is counsel's reading. Your part is the technical condition that would trigger it, and whether you saw that condition controlled. You do not compute their loss. You do not advise them to sign the financing.

Obsolescence is the same shape. The supply agreement either names what happens if the generation cannot be supported, or it does not. You say whether the technical assumption is written. You do not predict the market for the generation. A comparison to other halls you have seen is not evidence. Another hall is not this pack.

## Decisions to record

- The performance sentence, quoted short, or "not stated."
- Evidence for each assumption inside it: seen, document, or absent.
- Legal remedies left to counsel. No loss calculated.
