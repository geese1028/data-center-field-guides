# 12. Red flags, then the report

If the engagement asks for an early flag list, it is a list of what would change the financing sentence, known so far, not a draft of the whole report. A flag is a missing permit counsel needed, a performance sentence with no evidence, a chair that cannot be named, a sample that already failed, a path that is only a speech. A messy cable is not a flag unless it is the path.

The report that follows has one opinion, narrow. Scope. What you read. What you saw. What you accepted as their document. What you did not look at. Findings that change a delivery node or later operation, each with the evidence tag. Open items. The sentence you will sign, and the sentence you will not.

"No issue found" is allowed only with the look attached. No issue in the rooms you entered, on the sample you wrote, in the documents you opened. A bare no-issue is how the unread appendix becomes reviewed.

Their comment on the draft is a comment. It does not delete a finding. It may add a document you then read and tag. If the document closes the finding, write the close with the revision. If it does not, the finding stays. Do not soften the verb to keep the timetable. A late document is a new read, not a silent edit of the opinion you already sent.

The opinion does not certify the tenant's business, the market, or the legal enforceability of the contract. Say that in the same section as the yes. A yes without those limits will be quoted without them.

## Decisions to record

- Flags limited to what changes the financing sentence.
- No-issue sentences, each with the look attached.
- A comment that tried to delete a finding, and whether a new document actually closed it.
- Limits in the same section as the opinion.
