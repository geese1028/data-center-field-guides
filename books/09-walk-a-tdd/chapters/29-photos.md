# 29. A photograph is not a finding

You will take pictures, or you will be handed theirs. A photograph shows that a thing was in front of a lens at a time. It does not show that the plant meets the contract, that a load is the load, or that a screen is live.

If the engagement allows photographs, write the rule before you raise the camera. What may be shot. What may leave their building. What may enter the report. Serial plates, screens of their jobs, and drawings on the wall are identifiers. They stay in the working file if they are allowed at all. They do not enter a report that will be forwarded, unless the engagement says that exact image may be forwarded. When in doubt, leave it out. A finding can be written without the picture. If it cannot, you have a caption, not a finding.

A photo of a nameplate is not a measurement of load, flow, or capacity. A photo of a green tile is not a quality mark. A photo of a closed door is not a review of the room behind it. A photo they staged, with panels on and alarms cleared, is their scene. Tag it as their scene if you use it. Do not tag it as your walk.

Time matters. A photograph with no clock, or with a clock you do not trust, cannot support "as of the visit" by itself. Your note has the day and the room. The image illustrates the note. It does not replace it.

Do not send a contact sheet to the lender as an appendix of proof. An appendix of pictures will be read as the opinion. Choose none, or choose the few that a sentence already explains, with the limits in the caption. No caption, no picture.

## Photographic Evidence Hygiene: Nameplates, Gauge States, and Redaction Rules

In institutional M&A acquisitions and project finance underwriting for mission-critical data centers, every qualitative observation recorded during the site walk must be translated into a deterministic commercial or contractual parameter for the investment committee and lending syndicate:

$$\text{Valid Audit Photo} = \{\text{Engraved Tag ID}, \text{Live Meter Value}, \text{Timestamp}\}$$

An observation that merely remarks on plant appearance without quantifying its impact on **Firm Deliverable IT Capacity ($\text{MW}$)**, **Single-Point-of-Failure ($\text{SPOF}$) Availability**, **Immediate Catch-Up Capital Expenditure ($\text{CapEx}$)**, or **Conditions Precedent ($\text{CP}$) / Conditions Subsequent ($\text{CS}$)** fails to protect the relying party.

## Decisions to record

- What may be photographed, written before the walk.
- Identifiers kept out of the forwarded report.
- Staged scenes tagged as theirs.
- No picture without a sentence that does not need the picture.
