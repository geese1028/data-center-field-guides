# 23. Their test is not your test

Performance will often rest on a document the customer or the operator already signed: a factory record, an on-site acceptance, a platform test they ran the week before you arrived. You were not there for most of it. The honest verb is accepted as a document, unless you stood there and saw the step.

Read the document for the same gates you would have wanted if you had written it. A scope. A date. A revision of what was tested. An expected result that existed before the run, or a result that is only a plot. Who signed. Whether failures were listed or the page says none. A page that says none while the body lists deviations is a false document. You do not adopt it. You finding it.

You do not rerun the suite to be helpful. A partial rerun you invent in the window is an unapproved test. It will be remembered as your acceptance of the whole suite. Either witness what they are running while you are there, under their script, or accept the document with its limits, or say performance was not shown. Three choices. A demonstration they stage for you, outside the script, is the fourth, and you refuse it as a test. The handover book already named that demonstration. It is more tempting when a lender is waiting for a sentence.

If the document covers one cluster and the contract covers the hall, the sentence narrows to the cluster. Do not let their title, which says the project, widen your verb. Titles lose. The scope line wins.

## Decisions to record

- Their performance document: revision, date, expected result present or absent.
- Your verb: witnessed, accepted as a document, or not shown.
- A staged demonstration you did not adopt.
- Scope of their test, not the title, used in your sentence.
