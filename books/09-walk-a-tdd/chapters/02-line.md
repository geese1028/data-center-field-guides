# 2. The line you will not cross

A hall visit collects everything, because everything is in the room. Your scope does not. Write the line before the questionnaire goes out. For an operator who delivers infrastructure and a hardware platform, the line often stops under the tenant's operating system. Applications, the tenant's own security operation, and the management of their jobs are not your audit, unless the engagement names them in a sentence you can evidence.

Crossing the line feels thorough. It makes the report wider than the access you had. A screenshot of a dashboard is not an application audit. A conversation about how the customer trains models is not a finding about the plant. File the conversation as context if you must. Do not let it support a sentence about fitness.

The same line runs the other way. The room provider's electrical plant is not the operator's spare-parts drawer, and the operator's drawer is not the tenant's acceptance test. Each chair has papers. You do not borrow a pass from one chair to clear another. Chapter 3 names the chairs. This chapter only says you write the line, and you repeat it in the report so a later reader cannot widen it.

If the engagement is silent, ask before you travel. Silence is not permission to audit the tenant. It is a missing scope. A missing scope is refused the way a missing sheet is refused. You may still walk what you can see. You may not call the unseen layers reviewed.

## When they widen it for you

The party under review will offer more access than the engagement, because more access feels like a cleaner opinion. A tenant screen, a security log, a job history. Take it only if you are willing to evidence it and the reader asked. Extra access you do not know how to judge will be described badly, and the description will be treated as a review. Decline, and write declined. Declined is cleaner than a paragraph of impressions.

If the reader later wishes the line had been wider, that is a new engagement, with access and a method, not a sentence you add because you happened to see a screen. Happened to see is not a sample rule. It is how one machine becomes "the platform."

## Decisions to record

- The lowest layer in scope, and the first layer out of scope.
- Access you do not have. Those layers are not reviewed.
- A dashboard you will not treat as an application audit.
