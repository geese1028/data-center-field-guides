# 24. Do not smooth a contradiction

The visit produces contradictions. The questionnaire says the spare is on site. The shelf does not have it. The roster says four people. The interview can name one. The drawing says two feeds. The operator says one of them has been open for months. The contract says a response time. The emergency paper cannot be found at the panel.

Each contradiction is a finding, or it is a limit on a sentence. It is not a puzzle for you to solve in the hotel by choosing the likely one. Likely is how their story becomes your opinion. Write both sides, with tags. Seen beats a document when they disagree about what is in the room today. A signed contract beats an interview about what is owed. An interview beats a slide about whether the person can do the job tonight. Say which rule you used. Do not mix them to reach a comfortable middle.

Do not fix the contradiction by helping them edit the questionnaire before you leave, so that the file matches the shelf. That edit is their change to the claim. Your finding remains the claim as it was when you asked, unless you reopen the question and write that you did. A cleaned questionnaire dated the morning you arrived, handed over at noon, is a story. Stories do not close findings.

If you cannot tell which side is real, the sentence that needed the fact is refused. Not knowing is allowed. Averaging is not. The report can say the papers disagree and the performance sentence is not evidenced. That is a complete finding. It does not need a winner.

## Decisions to record

- Both sides of each contradiction, tagged.
- Which source wins for that question, and why.
- A questionnaire edited after the walk, not treated as the original claim.
- A sentence refused because the sides could not be told apart.
