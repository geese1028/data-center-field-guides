# 16. What this book does not own

It does not own the test sequence. The third book owns the claim, the scripts, the levels, the punch register, and the short pack the tester hands over. This book owns the receiver's refusal. If a chapter here starts to tell you how to run the heat window, cut it. Point at the third book and keep the pen on the table.

It does not own the file judgment, the measurement method, the day sheet, or the point sheet. It applies them at a signature. A reader who needs the test itself goes to those books. Do not paste their sheets into this one and fill them with a fictional hall.

It does not own a code, a facilities standard, or a commissioning guideline. Titles may be cited when a contract already names them. The text is not pasted and not digested. A limit you remember from a slide is not a limit you may put in a signature. ASHRAE has said its publication content is not to be entered into an AI tool, and that AI derivative works of that content need written permission. This book does not enter that content.

It does not own a customer's handover procedure, a point list, a spare catalog, a training deck, or a shift log. Those stay in their file. A daily operations handover is not this meeting. Do not use one to prove the other.

It does not own a real meeting. Chapter 14 is a method. It is not a case you may quote as a project. No site, no person, no number you did not measure.

It does not own the repair, the commercial claim, or the schedule. Those may be urgent. They do not convert missing evidence into present evidence. Record them on a different page, under a different name.

## Decisions to record

- The sequence you did not reteach.
- The code, the procedure, and the point list you did not paste.
- The real meeting you did not write down.
