# 8. An open line is not a signature

The third book owns how a punch register is written: a line a stranger can understand, a party who will fix it, evidence that closes it, no next stage on an open fail, a waiver that says what you will do if the defect appears under heat. You do not reteach that. You read the print. If there is no print, chapter 3 already refused the pack. If there is a print, this chapter says which open lines block your signature.

An open fail on a script they claim as proved blocks the proved-sentence for that script. It does not necessarily block a narrower sentence about a different system. Do not let one open line poison a scope that is actually closed, and do not let a closed scope launder an open line. Two sentences. The third book said outstanding work goes on page two. You check that page two is the same list as the print. A page two that says none, while the print has open lines, is a false document. Refuse the page, not just the line.

## Waivers you will live with

![The Punch List Defect Triad Matrix](../figures/fig-02-punchlist-defect-triad-matrix.png)


A waiver without the sentence "what we will do if this appears" is not a waiver. It is permission to be surprised. You are the person who will be surprised. If you cannot operate with the defect, you refuse the waiver even if the owner of the project is willing to sign it. Their willingness is the tester's or the owner's hat. Yours is the operator's. The third book allowed the owner to witness a waiver. It did not require the operator to pretend the defect is acceptable.

A waiver that says "we will watch it" is not a sentence. Watch with which point, on which clock, and what you do when it appears. If the point was never accepted under the sixth book, you cannot watch it. The waiver fails twice. Refuse.

Closing a line in the meeting, from a verbal assurance, is not a close. The third book required the same evidence the script asked for the first time. A letter that says the setting was adjusted is not a changeover. You do not accept the close. You also do not perform the retest in the meeting. The line stays open. The signature that needed it stays refused.

## Questions still open

An open question to the designer, the vendor, or the tester is an open line if your sentence depends on the answer. It does not matter that they filed it in a different tracker from the punch. If the answer changes a stop, a setpoint, a capacity, or which point is allowed to speak, you cannot sign past it. Ask them to put it on page two, or put it on your sheet yourself and refuse the sentence that needed the answer.

A question they call closed because someone replied in a mail, and the reply is not in the pack, is not closed. Chapter 11. A document you cannot open is not a document. The same rule applies to a reply. If the second person cannot find it, the question is open.

Do not answer the question yourself in the meeting to unblock the signature. An answer you invent is a new design, and you are not wearing that hat. Leave it open. Narrow the sentence so it does not need the answer, or sign nothing.

## Closed lines you still read

A line closed on a photograph may be enough for a label. It is not enough for a failed changeover, a leak, or a protection point. If the register treats those the same, the close is not evidence. The line is open for your purposes. Write that on your sheet so the print's "closed" and your "not accepted" both exist. You are not rewriting their register. You are refusing to inherit a bad close.

Defects found after they left the hall are still defects. If you already know of one, it goes in the register before you sign, with the date you learned it. Signing first and adding it after is how your own finding becomes a favor instead of a condition. Put it on page two before the pen.

## Decisions to record

- Open lines that block a proved-sentence, named by function, not by a site code.
- Page two matched to the print, or the page refused.
- Waivers you refused because you cannot operate them, or because the watch-point cannot speak.
- Closes you did not inherit: photograph used where a witnessed retest was required.
- Your own known defect, on page two before you sign.
- Open questions your sentence depends on, treated as open lines. The answer you did not invent in the room.
