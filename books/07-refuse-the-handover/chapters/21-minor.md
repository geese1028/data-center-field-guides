# 21. Minor is not a size

The word minor will be used on the open line, the missing sheet, and the unaccepted point. It is not a size you can see. It is a request that you treat a gate as a delay. Chapter 12 already split those. This chapter is how the request is phrased, so you hear it before you answer it.

Minor means they want the wide sentence, with the defect left on page two in softer language. Typical means another project signed, and they want that signature to be evidence. Industry practice means a habit, not a sheet. We'll live with it means you will live with it. The other hall signed means nothing about this pack. Chapter 22. None of these phrases puts a file judgment, a walked point, or a forced failure into the room. You answer the phrase by naming the gate, not by debating the adjective.

## How to answer

Repeat the gate in one line. "Heat-proved needs the file judgment. It is not here. That sentence is refused." Do not add a ranking of how serious the gap feels. A ranking is how minor gets back in. If they ask whether you can operate tonight without that sentence, answer from chapter 12: only inside the sentence you can actually sign. If that is nothing, say nothing. Do not invent a small operation so the answer sounds cooperative.

If they offer to write minor on the punch line, refuse the word. The line keeps the function, the expected result, and what happened, which is the third book's rule for a line a stranger can understand. Minor is not a description. It is a conclusion, and it is yours only if you can operate with the defect and you write what you will do when it appears. If you cannot, the line is open, and it is not minor. Their pen does not get to enter your conclusion.

## Bundles of small gaps

Several gaps they call small are sometimes one refusal. A missing judgment, a page two that says none, and a certificate in the wrong slot are not three minors. They are a pack you cannot sign. Do not negotiate them one by one into conditions if each is a gate. Conditions are for evidence that may arrive late. Gates are for evidence the sentence needs now. A meeting that turns every gate into a condition has talked you out of the book. Stop and read chapter 12 again before you initial anything.

A single typo, a name spelled two ways, a tab numbered out of order: those can be small, and you may ignore them if they do not change a sentence. Write that you ignored them, in one line, so a later reader does not think you did not notice. Ignoring a typo is not ignoring an open changeover. Say which is which. If you cannot tell, it is not a typo. It is a line you have not understood, and an understood line is the third book's minimum. Ununderstood is open. Chapter 8.

## Decisions to record

- The adjective they used, and the gate you named in reply.
- Minor struck from any line you did not agree to live with.
- Gates you refused to convert into a bundle of conditions.
- Typos you ignored, listed, so they are not confused with open lines.
