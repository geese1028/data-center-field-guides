# 0. How to use this book

This book is for the person who is being asked to sign that a hall may be operated. The pack on the table may be thick. The question is narrower. Does the sentence on the signature match the evidence, and are you the person who will live with what is missing?

It is not a commissioning course. It is not a construction acceptance code. It is not a customer's handover procedure rewritten in cleaner English. The third book already teaches how to finish a verification and hand over a short pack. This book starts when that pack, or a longer one, is put in front of the person who will run the building. You do not rerun their scripts. You decide what you will not sign.

The earlier books supply the tests you apply. A file is not a pass until the second book allows the sentence. A model is not a measurement until the fourth book says how the points were taken. A screen does not speak until the sixth book has a clock, a quality mark, and a forced failure. A capacity cell is not a nameplate. This book does not reteach those tests. It says what you do when the pack fails one of them in a meeting.

When you finish, you should be able to leave the room with one refusal sheet. It names the sentence you will sign, the sentence you will not, and the open items that keep the second sentence closed. The blank sheet is in chapter 15. A meeting with no site and no borrowed numbers is in chapter 14. If the meeting is tomorrow, start at chapter 15, then read the chapter for the item they are pushing you to wave through. Drawings are chapter 18. Keys, tools, and what you are not taking custody of are chapter 19. The day after you refuse, or after you sign only the narrow page, is chapter 20. If they call the gap minor, or cite another hall, chapters 21 and 22. If you have the pack the day before, read it in the order in chapter 23.

## Who signs

The person on the hook is the one who will be asked, on a later morning, why the hall was accepted. That may be the operations manager. It may be an owner who will not operate, in which case they may sign only the sentence they actually control. They may not sign the operator's sentence for a person who has not read the pack.

A tester who signs their own close-out has finished the third book's job. That signature is not yours. Do not countersign it to be polite.

## What you may copy

You may copy the sheet headings. You may not paste a sentence from this book into a customer's procedure and call it their rule. Their contract, their code, and their point list stay in their file. If a code or a facilities title is already named in the contract, cite the edition. Do not paste it. Do not ask this book to remember a limit from it.

## Decisions to record

- The sentence they want you to sign, in their words, before you edit it.
- The sentence you are willing to sign, if it is narrower.
- The one item you will not wave through tomorrow.
