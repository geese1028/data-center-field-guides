# 19. Custody is not a compliment

Signing can be heard as taking the keys. Make sure the hearing is the sentence. Custody of a plant, custody of someone else's tools, and custody of a room full of drawings are three transfers. A wide "accepted" moves all three. You move only the one you mean.

## Keys and the right to stop

The operator pages name the stops. Custody includes whether you can reach the isolation those stops assume. A key, a permit, or a login that only the outgoing party holds means you cannot perform the stop. You have a tour, not an operated hall. Refuse the operated-sentence until a second person on your side can reach the same isolation, and the path is written where the night shift looks. Do not write the combination, the password, or the address in this book or in a pack that will be copied. Write that the path exists, who the two persons are, and where the secret is kept. The secret stays in the site file.

If the only way to stop the plant is to call a vendor, say that in the sentence. It is a limit, not a failure of manners. "We operate it" is false if you cannot isolate it. Chapter 9. A slide that showed the valve is not the valve in your hand.

A control output you did not accept under the sixth book is not a stop you own. Installed automatic action is not your procedure. Until a separate procedure is cited by title, not pasted, you do not sign that you operate through that output. You sign that it is not yours to command, or you refuse the page that implies it is.

## What is still on the floor

The third book put equipment return on the tester's close-out: machines and fittings go back against the unpack list. Your check is whether you are about to sign custody of tools that are not the hall's. A load bank, a hose, a meter, a temporary strainer, a bypass still installed: each is either returned, or left under a named owner who is not you, with an end date. If the signature is silent, the room will assume they are yours. Silence is acceptance. Write the list of what you are not taking, by function, not by a serial number you should not publish. Their serials stay in their file.

A temporary bypass left in place is a deviation. Chapter 8. It goes on the operator pages and on page two. It does not hide inside "minor remaining works." If you cannot operate with the bypass, you refuse custody of that loop. You may still refuse only that loop. Chapter 12. Do not take the building in order to be reasonable about a spool piece.

Consumables and chemicals follow the same split. You do not sign that you received a treatment program because a drum is in the room. A drum without a named owner, a sheet that says what it is for, and a stop if it is wrong, is an object, not a handover. Do not copy a treatment procedure into the pack or into this book. Cite their sheet by title if you accept it. If there is no sheet, you do not accept the program. The drum can stay under their custody until there is one.

## Drawings and backups, again

Chapter 18 took the drawing as evidence. This chapter takes it as an object you might be blamed for losing. If you accept custody of the as-built, you need the second person who can open the backup, chapter 3, and you need to know which revision you took. Accepting "the drawings" without a revision is accepting a pile. Piles are how the design PDF returns under an as-built name.

## Physical Key Control, LOTO Transfer, and Spare Parts Custody Audit

### Separating Plant Custody from Contractor Tool Storage and Access

In institutional data center project delivery, the transition from construction and commissioning (ASHRAE Guideline 0 / Guideline 202 Levels 1 through 5) to live 24/7 operations is governed by measurable engineering gates rather than schedule milestones. Whenever a handover package asks the receiving operations lead to certify a plant attribute, that attribute must satisfy the quantitative gating relationship:

$$N_{\text{spares\_on\_shelf}} \ge N_{\text{critical\_BOM}}, \quad \text{Access}_{\text{contractor}} = \text{Revoked}$$

If any supporting evidence file, witnessed test script, or as-built redline verification falls outside its acceptance corridor, the operations representative must either issue a formal **Notice of Handover Refusal** or execute a narrow **Conditional Acceptance with 200% Retainage Escrow** that explicitly bounds the permitted operating envelope until the defect is cured and re-tested.

| Handover Audit Dimension | Contractor / Project Team Submission | Operations Gatekeeper Verification Protocol | Mandatory Refusal or Escrow Action |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Primary Governance Focus** | Requiring Lock-Cylinder Re-Keying and Spare Inventory Count at Turnover | Independent line-by-line audit against Volumes 01–06 criteria | Strike unsupported words from certificate before signing |
| **Quantitative Proof Standard** | Summary pass/fail cover sheet or verbal assurance | Raw 1-second telemetry CSV, signed witness sheet, and field walk | Classify missing proof as Category A/B defect on Refusal Sheet |
| **Financial & Operational Lever** | Request for 100% milestone release and full load | Enforce $200\%$ retainage holdback ($E = 2.0 \times C_{\text{fix}}$) and load cap | Hold plant at zero or derated load until remediation passes |
| **Post-Meeting Custody Rule** | Unfettered contractor access to finish open work | Transition space to Operations LOTO; require approved MOP per fix | Deny unescorted access; log all post-handover interventions |

## Decisions to record

- Isolation a second person on your side can reach, or the operated-sentence refused.
- Automatic controls you did not take command of.
- Tools and temporary plant you are not taking, with the owner and the end date.
- A bypass written as a deviation, or that loop refused.
- A chemical program accepted only by title, or not accepted.
- Drawing custody tied to a revision, or not taken.
