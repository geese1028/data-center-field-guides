# 0. How to use this book

This book is for the person who is being asked to sign that a hall may be operated. The pack on the table may be thick. The question is narrower. Does the sentence on the signature match the evidence, and are you the person who will live with what is missing?

It is not a commissioning course. It is not a construction acceptance code. It is not a customer's handover procedure rewritten in cleaner English. The third book already teaches how to finish a verification and hand over a short pack. This book starts when that pack, or a longer one, is put in front of the person who will run the building. You do not rerun their scripts. You decide what you will not sign.

The earlier books supply the tests you apply. A file is not a pass until the second book allows the sentence. A model is not a measurement until the fourth book says how the points were taken. A screen does not speak until the sixth book has a clock, a quality mark, and a forced failure. A capacity cell is not a nameplate. This book does not reteach those tests. It says what you do when the pack fails one of them in a meeting.

When you finish, you should be able to leave the room with one refusal sheet. It names the sentence you will sign, the sentence you will not, and the open items that keep the second sentence closed. The blank sheet is in chapter 15. A meeting with no site and no borrowed numbers is in chapter 14. If the meeting is tomorrow, start at chapter 15, then read the chapter for the item they are pushing you to wave through. Drawings are chapter 18. Keys, tools, and what you are not taking custody of are chapter 19. The day after you refuse, or after you sign only the narrow page, is chapter 20. If they call the gap minor, or cite another hall, chapters 21 and 22. If you have the pack the day before, read it in the order in chapter 23.

## Who signs

The person on the hook is the one who will be asked, on a later morning, why the hall was accepted. That may be the operations manager. It may be an owner who will not operate, in which case they may sign only the sentence they actually control. They may not sign the operator's sentence for a person who has not read the pack.

A tester who signs their own close-out has finished the third book's job. That signature is not yours. Do not countersign it to be polite.

## What you may copy

You may copy the sheet headings. You may not paste a sentence from this book into a customer's procedure and call it their rule. Their contract, their code, and their point list stay in their file. If a code or a facilities title is already named in the contract, cite the edition. Do not paste it. Do not ask this book to remember a limit from it.

# 1. A signature is a sentence

People put three different acts under one word. A tester signs that a named scope was proved, not proved, or proved only for the systems named. An owner signs turnover for the scope that was actually run. An operator signs that they can run what was handed over, with the stops and the open items in view. This book is the third act. It may happen in the same meeting as the second. It is not the same sentence.

If the page in front of you says the building is commissioned, and the pack only ran a liquid verification in a finished hall, the page is wider than the evidence. You do not fix that by signing and adding a footnote nobody will read. You replace the sentence, or you refuse. A footnote under a wide sentence is how the wide sentence becomes the one the next person quotes.

Read the sentence before the meeting, not during the applause. If a lawyer needs to read it, the lawyer reads it before the meeting. The third book already said that about the tester's certificate. It is repeated here because the operator is the person most likely to be handed a pen at the end, when refusing feels like delay.

## What the sentence may say

A sentence you may sign names the scope, the date, and the limit. Proved for the systems named. Not proved for the levels that were not run. Open items listed, or an explicit none. The monitor accepted only for the points that passed a forced failure, or not accepted. The model accepted only as a picture, unless the measurement book was followed. Capacity left blank if the only number in the pack is a design value.

A sentence you may not sign says the hall is finished, the air is proved, the alarms are live, the staff are trained, or the capacity is the number on the drawing, unless each of those was evidenced in the way the earlier books require. Bundling them into "accepted" is the defect this book exists to stop.

Write your sentence in ink on the sheet, in words a stranger can compare to the page they asked you to sign. If the two do not match, you have not accepted their page. Say that in the meeting. Do not discover the mismatch in a later audit.

## Clocks you did not mean to start

A signature is sometimes the event that starts a warranty, a care period, or a payment. Those clocks are commercial. They are real, and they are not your evidence problem. Read them before the meeting so you are not surprised. Do not let them onto your sentence as the reason you accepted a missing sheet. If their page says acceptance and warranty in one paragraph, strike the acceptance words you cannot support and leave their commercial words for the person whose hat is commercial. Two pages are cleaner than one paragraph that makes a warranty depend on a lie about the file.

If you cannot strike, and the only page starts the clock only when you sign the wide sentence, you still do not sign the wide sentence. Their clock is their problem to redraft. Your name does not repair their form. Say that before the meeting, when there is time to split the page. Saying it during the applause is true and late. Late and true is still better than a false yes.

## Applause is not evidence

A meeting that ends on time is not a result. A pack that is heavy is not a result. A vendor who says the remaining items are minor has not closed them. Minor is a judgment you make after you have read the line, and only if you can operate with it open. If you cannot, it is not minor. It is a refusal.

Do not sign a page you have not read, including the attachments the page incorporates by reference. A sentence that says "including all appendices" incorporates the empty tabs too. Either you have seen them, or you strike the words. Striking words is allowed. Pretending you saw them is not.

# 2. You are not the tester

The third book owns the sequence: claim, people, scripts approved before the day, static checks, one system at a time, a no-heat curve, heat, shutdown, the short pack. If that work was not done, you do not do it in the handover meeting to save the signature. You refuse the sentence that pretends it was done. Running a quick demonstration so the room can sign is a new test, without a script, without a file judgment, and without the stops. It does not repair the pack. It adds an unapproved event that the next person will quote as acceptance.

Your hat in this meeting is reader and signer, or refuser. If you also wear the tester's hat, write that, and do not sign the operator's sentence in the same hour you are still judging files. The two acts need a pause. The file judgment is not honest when the pen is already in your hand.

## What you do not redo

You do not rebuild their punch register. You read it. A line you cannot understand is an open line, even if they marked it closed. You do not rewrite their level map. You check that the words on the certificate match the map. You do not walk the hall to create the measurement the model never had, unless a separate job has been opened for that walk, with time and a sheet. A walk you do as a favor, unrecorded, will be remembered as your acceptance of the model.

You do not accept a point list by scanning it. The sixth book already refused pasted lists. Your question is whether the points that will page you were accepted as sources. A thick list is not that acceptance.

You do not fill gaps in their pack with your own knowledge of the site. What you know and did not write down is not in the pack. The next operator does not have your memory. If the pack needs your memory to be true, the pack is not ready, and you are not allowed to be the missing appendix.

## What you may ask for

You may ask for the page that should already exist: the sentence of what was proved, the level map, the printed punch, the file judgment, the list of what was witnessed and what was accepted as a document, the operator pages. Asking is not testing. If they cannot produce the page in the meeting, the item is missing. Missing is a refusal of any sentence that needed that page. You do not give them until the end of the day to write it while you wait with the pen, unless you have left the meeting and opened a new one after you have read what they wrote. A page written while you wait is a story. Stories do not become evidence because they are fresh.

# 3. A missing sheet is a refusal

The third book already listed the short pack a liquid verification owes, and it already said an empty tab is a false document. This chapter is the receiver's use of that list. You are not collecting tabs to feel complete. You are checking that each sentence they want you to sign has the sheet that supports it. No sheet, no sentence.

You do not demand the long building list. Seasonal tests, fire integration, an enclosure test, a sampling essay: include them only if the claim included them. Demanding empty categories so your checklist looks like a code is the same sin as their empty tab. Cite a code by title if the contract already names it. Do not paste it, and do not invent its clauses from memory.

## The sheets that gate your signature

These are the sheets. A pack may have more. It may not have less, if the matching sentence is on the page.

The sentence of what was proved, not proved, or proved only for the systems named. Outstanding work on the next page, or an explicit none. A report that hides open items in an appendix is a brochure. You refuse the brochure's sentence.

The level map: issued, attached from earlier work, refused. The third book's numbering collision still applies. A local stage called Level 1 is not the factory certificate. If the map is missing, you cannot tell what you are signing. Refuse.

The file judgment, in the form the second book defined, for every heat result they want you to treat as proved. A plot is not that judgment.

The printed punch register. Empty of open items, or not empty, in ink. An oral "everything is fine" is not a print.

What was witnessed, and what was accepted as a document, for each script they claim. If you cannot tell those apart, you do not know whether anyone saw the plant.

The operator pages: what was tested, what was not, where the stops are, what deviation is still in force. Training, if they claim the staff can run it, is a signature that those pages were shown. A slide deck is not the signature.

Calibration, if a number in the pack depends on an instrument. A number with no calibration line is a number you do not use. You do not have to rerun the calibration. You have to see that it was recorded, or refuse the number.

## A sheet that answers a different question

Some pages are present and still missing, because they answer a question you did not ask. A shift handover from a hall that is already running is not this signature. It shows that someone sat a shift. It does not show that construction handed the plant over. Do not accept a stack of daily sheets as the close-out pack. They are a different meeting.

A code checklist with boxes ticked, and no witness note, is not the file judgment and not the measurement sheet. Ticks are not the second book's sentence. A factory report for a catalog model, not for the revision on the floor, is not attached earlier work. Chapter 10. A training agenda is not the operator pages. Chapter 9.

When the title is right and the content is a different document, write "wrong document" on your sheet, not "present." Present is how a thick pack defeats a reader. Wrong document is a missing sheet. The sentence that needed the right one is refused.

## Empty, late, and borrowed

An empty tab with a hopeful title is a missing sheet. A sheet dated the morning of the meeting, with no witness and no file behind it, is a missing sheet until you have had time to read it outside the room. A sheet borrowed from another hall, with the name covered, is a missing sheet. Covering a name does not make a record yours.

A backup location that only one person can open is a missing sheet for the next shift. The third book put backup locations in the pack. You check that a second person, named, can open them. If not, the pack exists only while the author is in the building. That is not a handover.

# 4. A file is not a pass

They will show you a curve. The curve is not the result. The result is the sentence the second book allows, after the channels, the clock, the dwell, the sign, and the heat story have been checked. If that sentence is not in the pack, you do not sign "heat proved." You may sign that a file exists. Those are different sentences. People will try to make you treat them as one.

You do not reteach the file judgment in the meeting. You ask for it. If the person who ran the test cannot point to it, the file has not been judged. An unjudged file is not a construction defect and it is not a pass. The third book already separated those owners. Your job is to keep them separate when someone wants a single signature to cover both.

## What you refuse even if the curve looks calm

A failed poll held forward and stamped with a new time. A chart plotted faster than the poll, with the repeats unmarked. Two clocks averaged so the lines sit on top of each other. A heat story that does not say where the heat went. A pass line copied from a slide rather than from the specification then in force. A file that starts when the curve becomes interesting, with the baseline minute already lost.

Any one of these is enough to refuse the proved-sentence. You do not need all of them. You also do not need to compute a new number in the meeting. Computing a new number is testing. You are not the tester in this hour.

If they offer a kinder summary than the judgment sheet, refuse the summary. The second book exists because kinder summaries are how a bad file becomes a result. Your signature on the summary would be the kinder sentence, with your name under it.

## What you may accept

You may accept a sentence that says the file was judged and the judgment was "not a valid test," with the reason left in the second book's words, not rewritten. That is an honest limit. It belongs on page two of the pack, as outstanding work or as a result that does not prove the plant. It does not belong under a heading that says pass.

You may accept a sentence that names the systems the valid file actually covered, and refuses the rest. A file on one loop is not a file on the hall. The scope letter should already say this. If the signature page is wider than the scope letter, strike the extra words.

## Do not convert a bad file into their fault

A bad clock is not automatically a punch item against the constructor. A real leak is not automatically a software story. If you sign a sentence that mixes them, you have chosen an owner for the next year, and you may have chosen the wrong one. Leave the file invalid. Leave the plant unproved. Open a separate line only when a valid file shows a plant failure. Until then, the refusal is of the sentence, not of a named party.

# 5. A picture is not a measurement

A model report in the pack is a picture until the fourth book’s conditions are met. The points were named as a person would walk them. Supply is not a room average. A cabinet inlet is not a tile in the aisle if the claim is about the cabinet. The report says what a result may be used for, and what it may not. If those sentences are missing, you do not sign "airflow proved," "the hall is cool," or any sentence that treats the picture as a measurement.

You do not open the model in the meeting and judge colors. Color is not a temperature. A scale they chose for the slide is not the specification. If they want you to look, look for the measurement sheet, not for the reddest rack.

## What you refuse

A report that cites a facilities title and then prints a limit this pack did not measure. You may not carry that limit out of their slide into your signature. ASHRAE titles may be named when the contract already names them. The text is not digested here, and it is not reconstructed from a picture.

A room average used as if it were the inlet the equipment sees. The fourth book already refused that substitution. Your refusal is of the sentence, even if the picture is beautiful.

A model dated before a change that moved a rack, a floor tile, or a setpoint, offered as the current hall. The picture is of a hall that is gone. Say so. Do not ask them to "update the colors" during the meeting and then sign. An update is a new report. You read it later.

A recommendation in the report that has already been built, with no walked check after the build. The model may have been right. The hall may not have followed it. Without the walk, you do not know which. You sign neither "built as modeled" nor "model accepted as the hall."

## What you may accept

You may accept the report as a picture, filed, not used for a pass. Write that limit in your sentence. A picture can still be useful to the next design. It is not useful as a substitute for the points you will be blamed for.

You may accept a measurement sheet that names the points, the instrument, the time, and the sentence those points support, and that says where it disagrees with the picture. Disagreement is not averaged away. The walked reading wins until the model is corrected, which is the same rule the sixth book used for a screen. A monitor is not above a walked reading. A model is not above it either.

If the measurement sheet is promised for next week, the airflow sentence waits until next week. You do not sign it now and add "subject to measurement." Subject to is how the sentence gets quoted without the subject.

# 6. A green tile is not a handover

The screen will be on in the meeting. Green is the claim the sixth book exists to test. You do not sign that the monitor is part of the handover because the tiles are green. You sign it only for points that have a source path, an owner, a clock, a quality mark you can explain, a line written before the night, and a forced failure on the image you will run. Everything else may be stored. It may not page you, and it may not be called accepted.

This is the receiver's use of the sixth book. You do not fill a point sheet for their whole list in the meeting. You pick the points the signature depends on: the ones that will wake someone, the ones that fill a capacity cell, the ones they say prove recovery. If those cannot pass the sheet, the monitor sentence is refused. The other points can wait. A complete list is not the gate. The points that speak are the gate.

## What you refuse

A point whose forced failure was done on a vendor's hall, or on last quarter's image. Acceptance is this image, this path. A rename since the proof voids it. If they cannot say whether the path changed, treat it as changed.

A quality mark nobody in the room can define in one sentence. Those points are missing for any decision that wakes a person. Do not let the meeting define the mark by agreeing the tile looks fine.

A score that will be used to say the hall is healthy, the incident is closed, or the customer may be told the service is held. The score does not get a pen. If the handover sentence needs the score, refuse the sentence. You do not need their weights. You need the refusal.

# 14. One meeting, as a method

This chapter walks one meeting so the sheet has a shape. Nothing in it is a site. There is no hall name, no reading, no borrowed log, and no code clause. Where a number would have been, the text says "the line they wrote" or "the cell they want filled." If your meeting has those numbers, they stay in your file. They do not belong here.

Four items are on the table. A heat file with a calm curve and no judgment sentence. A model report with no walked points. A screen that is green, on an image nobody failed on purpose. A punch print with one open line on a changeover, and a page two that says none. They ask you to sign that the hall is commissioned and ready to operate.

## The hat

You are not the tester. You do not rerun the heat window in the room. You do not walk the hall as a favor. You do not click the tiles until one of them looks convincing. You ask for the four sheets: the file judgment, the measurement sheet, the forced-failure record, the punch print against page two.

The file judgment is not in the pack. The curve stays a curve. You refuse the proved-sentence for heat. You do not open a punch line against a constructor for a bad file. Two facts, both unproved.

The measurement sheet is not in the pack. You accept the model as a picture, filed, not used. You refuse every sentence that treats it as the hall's air. You do not copy a limit off the slide.

The forced failure is not in the pack. The screen is installed. It is not accepted. The points that would wake a person stay silent. The score, if they mention it, does not enter your sentence. Design kilowatts stay out of the capacity cell. The cell stays blank.

The punch print and page two disagree. Page two is a false document. You refuse any signature that points at it. The open changeover blocks the proved-sentence for that script. A verbal close in the room is not a close. You do not witness a retest you did not plan. The line stays open.

## The sentences you actually sign

You do not sign their page. You sign a narrower page, or you sign nothing. In this meeting the honest narrow page is short. Named systems were not proved for heat, because the file was not judged. The model is filed as a picture. The screen is installed and not accepted. The punch page that says none is rejected. Outstanding work is the print, not page two. Levels not shown on a map are not claimed. The word commissioned is struck.

If they will not put that refusal in the same document as any yes, there is no yes. You leave the page unsigned. Unsigned is the result. It is not a failure of manners.

You do not add "subject to review next week" on the heat sentence. The missing judgment is a gate, not a delay. Chapter 12. You may date a later meeting. The date is not an acceptance. You also do not let them keep your name on a circulated draft of their original page "for attendance." Attendance is a list. A draft with your name under their sentence will be treated as a yes. Take your name off before you leave the room.

## If they split the ask

Sometimes the room, hearing the refusal, offers a smaller sentence on the spot. Read it the same way you read the first one. A smaller sentence can still smuggle the score, the design kilowatt, or the word commissioned. Strike those even on the small page. A smaller sentence that still points at page two is still pointing at a false document. Refuse the pointer.

If the smaller sentence is actually the narrow page you were willing to sign, put the refusals in the same document before you sign it. Chapter 12. Do not sign the small page now and attach the refusals after lunch. After lunch the small page will already have been copied without them.

If two people ask you to sign two different small sentences, chapter 24 applies. Your name goes on one sentence, the one you can support. It does not go on both as a courtesy. Courtesy is how the second sentence acquires a witness it did not earn.

## What this meeting was for

You leave with their sentence copied, your sentence written, four refusals, a blank capacity cell, and no root cause, no temperature, and no site. The next reader of your sheet can see what you refused without opening their slides. That is the handover record. Their pack, unchanged, is still their pack.

# 19. Custody is not a compliment

Signing can be heard as taking the keys. Make sure the hearing is the sentence. Custody of a plant, custody of someone else's tools, and custody of a room full of drawings are three transfers. A wide "accepted" moves all three. You move only the one you mean.

A key, a permit, or a login that only the outgoing party holds means you cannot perform the stop. You have a tour, not an operated hall. Refuse the operated-sentence until a second person on your side can reach the same isolation, and the path is written where the night shift looks. Do not write the combination, the password, or the address in this book or in a pack that will be copied. Write that the path exists, who the two persons are, and where the secret is kept. The secret stays in the site file.

If the only way to stop the plant is to call a vendor, say that in the sentence. It is a limit, not a failure of manners. "We operate it" is false if you cannot isolate it. Chapter 9. A slide that showed the valve is not the valve in your hand.

# 21. Minor is not a size
