# A nameplate is not used capacity

![A nameplate does not fill the used cell.](figures/kw.png)

*Original diagram. Not a site record.*


The capacity cell needs a source, a date, and a unit, or it stays blank. A nameplate, a design kilowatt, and a redundancy graphic are not that number. They are claims about what was drawn. Today's load is a measurement, and only if the point is live.

Blank is allowed. It is better than a design number that will be quoted for a year. Write why it is blank: the measured point is missing, stale, or never in the claim. Do not add design and measured loads into one total on a whiteboard. That total is a new calculation. It does not get signed in the corridor.

Redundancy is the same trap. N+1 on a slide does not tell you what you can still serve if one machine is down tonight. Unless a test showed the remaining capacity with that machine actually out, the redundancy sentence is a design sentence. Note it beside the cell. Do not put it inside.

A future IT load is a plan. Plans do not fill the used cell. They do not fill the available cell unless you can explain the method in one sentence and operate to it.
